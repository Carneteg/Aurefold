AUREFOLD — BOOK ONE

THE BELL OF SILENCE

In the high silent valleys, a goatherd hears a voice. House Whitehart exists to test those who claim to hear.

MANUSCRIPT COMPLETE · COMING

Cover — The Bell of Silence

Where it begins

It begins with a testing. In the Light Heights — the high, silent valleys of the northwest — a goatherd named Sela hears a voice, or does not. Her house will come to call her the Called. The testing falls to Brother Tomas, the Tester, a man carrying the one thing a tester should not own: his first doubt.

Above them both stands Mother Alaine, fifty years at the griffes, head of the listening house.

The shape of the book

The Bell of Silence is a completed developmental draft: a prologue and thirty-four chapters, told in three movements, with two interludes in the voice of Mother Alaine — a woman whose survival is an archive no chronicle has rivaled. It opens outside the valley, then narrows to a single testing; from there the larger realm presses in — the Ten Great Houses, the coming jubilee, and the pressures already moving beyond the mountains.

In the second movement, a herald of House Blackcrest comes to stay in the valley, gathering the realm’s testimony for the coming 400-year jubilee — a chronicler writing down memories that, until now, only one woman has kept.

The book asks one question, and does not answer it: what do you do with a voice you cannot verify? It closes where the charts do — at the edge of the map, with someone still walking.

In the end she turns east. What turned her is the one thing the book will not confirm.
