# Aurefold — Character Fandom Loops v1.0

**Status:** APPROVED NON-CANON MARKETING / COMMUNITY FORMAT  
**Scope:** Sela, Tomas, Alaine  
**Purpose:** build character attachment before encyclopedic lore familiarity.  
**Authority:** downstream of the active Aurefold source hierarchy, Book One Master v1.7, Book One Character, Voice & Rhythm Canon v1.0, and Character Appearance Bible stack. This file creates no story canon and no new character dialogue canon.

## Strategic objective

Aurefold should produce readers who say **“Tomas is my favourite,” “I understand Sela,” “Alaine terrifies me because she is sometimes right”** before they can explain the Tenfold Compact.

The social account therefore needs recurring character formats with recognizable emotional and verbal DNA.

These are not meme skins pasted over the cast. Each loop must preserve the character as a full person with appetites, fatigue, status, work, private agency, mistakes and contradictions.

## Shared rules

1. **Never invent a line and present it as a quotation from the novel.** Editorial paraphrases must not use quotation marks in a way that implies manuscript text.
2. Character social copy may exaggerate a recognizable tendency for clarity, but never contradict the governing character controls.
3. At least one post in every three for a character must reveal vulnerability, private motive, bodily reality, cost or error — not only the recurring joke.
4. No character may become the official moral voice of Aurefold.
5. No format may resolve protected mysteries, prove magic, establish the first aggressor at the Gate, reconcile Nine / Twelve, resolve Col, or explain the Bell.
6. Avoid modern meme slang that breaks Aurefold's literary identity. Dry wit is welcome; disposable internet voice is not.
7. Visual continuity must use the active appearance controls. Unsupported costume, jewelry, scars, weapons, symbols or mystical effects do not become canon through social art.
8. Preferred length: 12–30 seconds for a short; 3–6 cards for a carousel; one strong thought per post.
9. Every social link uses a source key so Community Manager can compare entry → Ledger participation. Character fandom is measured by action and return, not views alone.
10. The account may ask “what would you do?” but must not state that the character's likely choice is objectively correct.

# 1. SELA — **SELA HAS NO PATIENCE FOR…**

## Character truth being amplified

Sela is concrete, bodily, pragmatic and earthy. Her intelligence lives in work, cost, weather, animals, hunger, hands, weight and consequence. Her dry irony lands hardest when institutions turn simple material realities into self-important abstractions.

**Primary danger:** turning her into a generic sarcastic rebel or an anti-religion mouthpiece.

Sela is not “the woman who mocks everything.” She can care deeply, fear, need, protect, avoid and make compromised choices. Her skepticism is strongest when she sees the physical price hidden behind elegant language.

## Visual recognition

Use selectively:
- red, callused working hands;
- practical brown braid;
- wind-browned skin;
- thin white notch through left eyebrow;
- compact working competence;
- cloth, bowl, rope, goat-work, dirt or other materially supported work context.

Do not glamorize her into an immaculate fantasy heroine.

## Repeatable structure

**Card / beat 1:** `SELA HAS NO PATIENCE FOR…`  
**Beat 2:** one institutional abstraction.  
**Beat 3:** the physical thing that abstraction is hiding.  
**Beat 4:** dry editorial sting.  
**Beat 5:** reader question or Ledger CTA.

### Good subjects
- a committee naming hunger instead of feeding someone;
- a procedure that asks the same practical question twice;
- a ceremonial explanation that ignores the animal, body, weather or broken object in front of it;
- people arguing about meaning while someone still has work to finish;
- certainty claimed by people who do not carry its cost.

### Bad subjects
- “Sela hates religion.”
- “Sela is always right.”
- “Sela destroys everyone with savage comebacks.”
- modern snark detached from material reality.

## Recurring post templates

### Sela template A — Abstract word / concrete cost

**Hook:**  
`SELA HAS NO PATIENCE FOR: “ACCEPTABLE LOSS.”`

**Body:**  
`Acceptable to whom? Someone still has to lift the body, mend the gate, feed the animal, bury the dead, or explain the missing chair at supper.`

**End:**  
`Aurefold has many words for necessity. Sela tends to ask who carries it.`

**Prompt:**  
`What official phrase makes a real human cost sound suspiciously clean?`

### Sela template B — The practical objection

**Hook:**  
`A council has spent an hour debating what the broken bridge means.`

**Body:**  
`Sela would probably begin by asking whether anyone brought timber.`

**Rule:** this is an editorial characterization, not a canonical quote or event.

### Sela template C — Humanizing counter-post

**Hook:**  
`Pragmatism is easy when the cost belongs to someone else.`

**Body:**  
`Sela's strength is not that she never compromises. It is that Aurefold keeps making compromise physical: food, work, injury, shelter, another person's need. Practical choices can still leave a stain.`

**Prompt:**  
`When does survival stop being a complete defense of a choice?`

## Attribution convention

`character-sela-<theme>`

Examples:
- `character-sela-acceptable-loss`
- `character-sela-timber`
- `character-sela-survival-cost`

# 2. TOMAS — **TOMAS WOULD TECHNICALLY OBJECT…**

## Character truth being amplified

Tomas is exact, testing-minded, corrective, pedantic and dry. He is often unintentionally funny because he applies precision where other people want convenience, rhetoric or emotional closure.

**Primary danger:** writing him as a polished philosopher, omniscient logician, robotic bureaucrat or intentional stand-up comedian.

His humor comes from seriousness. The audience laughs because Tomas really does care about the distinction.

## Visual recognition

Use selectively:
- charcoal-marked fingers;
- long narrow hands;
- dark hair greying/receding unevenly;
- close beard kept with irritating precision;
- trial notes, margins, corrected marks, lamp or paper;
- controlled posture that can fail under pressure.

Do not make his visual precision slapstick.

## Repeatable structure

**Beat 1:** a confident statement.  
**Beat 2:** `TOMAS WOULD TECHNICALLY OBJECT.`  
**Beat 3:** the missing distinction.  
**Beat 4:** why the distinction has consequences.  
**Beat 5:** reader question / Ledger CTA.

### Good subjects
- “We know what happened” vs “we know what was recorded.”
- “Nobody objected” vs “no objection survives in the record.”
- “The numbers disagree” vs “the numbers count different things.”
- “No evidence” vs “evidence does not permit this conclusion.”
- a word that quietly changes legal or moral responsibility.

### Bad subjects
- random pedantry about spelling or grammar with no human consequence;
- making him always smarter than everyone;
- turning him into a meme accountant;
- using exactness to resolve Aurefold's required ambiguity.

## Recurring post templates

### Tomas template A — The forbidden shortcut

**Hook:**  
`“There is no evidence.”`

**Turn:**  
`TOMAS WOULD TECHNICALLY OBJECT.`

**Body:**  
`No evidence? Or no evidence that permits the conclusion you want? Those are different statements, and institutions become dangerous when convenience edits out the second half.`

**Prompt:**  
`Which distinction sounds pedantic until someone's life depends on it?`

### Tomas template B — Nine / Twelve crossover

**Hook:**  
`Nine and twelve do not agree.`

**Turn:**  
`Tomas's problem is more irritating: they do not claim to count the same thing.`

**Body:**  
`A contradiction cannot be repaired by pretending two questions were one.`

**Hard rule:** preserve the protected Nine bodies / Twelve missing separation.

### Tomas template C — Humanizing counter-post

**Hook:**  
`Precision does not save you from responsibility.`

**Body:**  
`Tomas can refuse an unsupported conclusion and still leave another person living inside the consequences of that refusal. Exactness protects against one kind of violence. It does not erase every other kind.`

**Prompt:**  
`Can refusing to judge become a judgment of its own?`

## Attribution convention

`character-tomas-<theme>`

Examples:
- `character-tomas-no-evidence`
- `character-tomas-nine-twelve`
- `character-tomas-refusal-cost`

# 3. ALAINE — **ALAINE JUDGES…**

## Character truth being amplified

Alaine brings hierarchy, judgment, intelligence, dignity and ritual competence. Her irony can be satirically condescending. She understands institutions deeply enough to see weaknesses others miss — and to overestimate the wisdom of the structures she knows how to operate.

**Primary danger:** flattening her into “mean old woman,” infallible matriarch, mystical crone or exposition priestess.

Alaine is compelling when authority, perception, age, fragility, amusement and overconfidence occupy the same body.

## Visual recognition

Use selectively:
- iron-white hair braided flat;
- large expressive eyes;
- walking stick;
- physical stillness others may misread as serenity or holiness;
- small movements carrying status;
- the eyes revealing judgment before speech.

Never present her as literally holy or supernaturally imposing.

## Repeatable structure

**Beat 1:** a person/institution performs importance.  
**Beat 2:** `ALAINE JUDGES.`  
**Beat 3:** identify the hidden status game, weakness or hypocrisy.  
**Beat 4:** turn the blade: Alaine's own framework may have a blind spot too.  
**Beat 5:** reader question.

### Good subjects
- ceremony used to disguise panic;
- people invoking tradition only when it benefits them;
- authority confusing obedience with agreement;
- novices performing certainty for senior eyes;
- competent institutions doing real good while protecting themselves.

### Bad subjects
- generic insults about appearance/intelligence;
- “Alaine is never impressed.”
- supernatural intuition;
- treating age as automatic moral wisdom.

## Recurring post templates

### Alaine template A — Performed certainty

**Hook:**  
`Someone says “there can be no doubt” before anyone has finished asking questions.`

**Turn:**  
`ALAINE JUDGES.`

**Body:**  
`Not necessarily because they are wrong. Because confidence performed too early is often an attempt to make rank do the work of evidence.`

**Prompt:**  
`When does authority become part of the evidence people think they saw?`

### Alaine template B — Tradition

**Hook:**  
`A rule has existed for generations.`

**Body:**  
`Alaine would respect that fact enough to ask the question modern cynics often skip: what problem did the rule once solve? She would also be capable of missing the second question: who is paying for it now?`

**Prompt:**  
`When does inherited wisdom become inherited cost?`

### Alaine template C — Humanizing counter-post

**Hook:**  
`Stillness can look like certainty from across a room.`

**Body:**  
`Alaine's authority is partly the discipline of not showing every thought at once. The people around her may call that serenity. It can also be pain, calculation, fatigue, pride, or simply the habit of being watched.`

**Prompt:**  
`How much of authority is what other people project onto composure?`

## Attribution convention

`character-alaine-<theme>`

Examples:
- `character-alaine-certainty`
- `character-alaine-tradition`
- `character-alaine-stillness`

# CROSSOVER FORMAT — THREE PEOPLE, ONE PROBLEM

Use sparingly. The purpose is to sharpen difference, not reduce the cast to a personality chart.

## Structure

**Problem:** one practical/institutional dilemma.  
**Sela notices:** the material cost or immediate work.  
**Tomas notices:** the unsupported inference or imprecise category.  
**Alaine notices:** the hierarchy, performance or institutional consequence.  
**Reader:** decides which question they would ask first.

### Example

**Problem:** A crowd is told that an official decision is “necessary.”

- **Sela:** necessary for whom, and who is carrying it?
- **Tomas:** necessary is a conclusion; what premises support it?
- **Alaine:** who gains authority by making necessity sound impersonal?

**Prompt:**  
`Which question would you ask first?`

This is editorial characterization, not canonical dialogue.

# CONTENT MIX

Recommended character cadence inside broader Aurefold social output:

- 40% sharp recognizable character tendency;
- 30% vulnerability / cost / contradiction;
- 20% crossovers with Ledger Question, House Test or Nine / Twelve;
- 10% ordinary life / humor / work.

Do not let character formats consume the whole feed. They work because they coexist with story, contested history, House identity and actual reading.

# CTA LOGIC

Use the CTA that best matches the post:

- moral disagreement → current Ledger Question;
- philosophical identity → House Test;
- character attachment → read Book One free;
- deeper creator/process material → free Patreon.

For measured community acquisition, prefer source-tagged URLs when the destination loads Aurefold community participation tracking.

# MODERATION

- Encourage disagreement about the character without treating dislike as failure to understand the book.
- Do not correct a fan for reading Alaine as arrogant, Tomas as frustrating or Sela as harsh when the reading is textually plausible.
- Correct invented canon claims.
- Do not let fandom sorting become “good people like X / bad people like Y.”
- Strong negative reactions can be valuable attachment signals if they engage with the actual character.

# SUCCESS MEASURES

Primary:
- comments naming the character rather than only the dilemma;
- repeat engagement with the same character format;
- source-attributed entry → Ledger / House Test action;
- readers voluntarily using character shorthand in later discussions;
- Book One reading clicks after character-led discovery.

Secondary:
- shares/saves;
- free Patreon intent;
- fan art or theories centered on the character.

A format has failed if the audience remembers the joke but not the person.
