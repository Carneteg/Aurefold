## Current dilemma

An official record has blamed one person for a disaster for years. New evidence now makes that conclusion doubtful. Many later judgments and settlements relied on the record, so correcting it publicly could reopen old disputes and weaken trust in the institution.

## Two defensible positions

### CORRECT THE RECORD NOW — integrity requires admitting what you no longer believe
An institution cannot ask people to trust its records while knowingly preserving a conclusion it now doubts. The person named in the record continues to carry a public judgment that may be false. Later decisions built on that record are the institution's responsibility to confront, not a reason to preserve a possible injustice.

### REVIEW BEFORE CORRECTION — responsibility includes the consequences of changing the record
Correcting one doubtful conclusion can unsettle many later judgments, settlements and lives. New evidence can itself be incomplete. If the institution reverses the record before understanding what follows, it risks replacing one uncertain account with another and creating fresh injustice in the name of repairing the first.
