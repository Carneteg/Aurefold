## Live dilemma

You guaranteed safe conduct to end a siege. After surrender, reliable evidence shows the opposing commander ordered grave acts you did not know about when you made the promise. Do you keep your word, or arrest the commander?

## Friday framing

**A promise can save lives. So can refusing to let it become immunity. Which obligation survives the surrender?**

### ARGUMENT A — HONOR THE SAFE CONDUCT

The value of safe conduct is not measured when keeping it is easy. It exists so an enemy can surrender without fearing that the terms will be rewritten afterward. If a guarantee can be revoked once the gates open, future commanders have a rational reason to fight longer — and more people may die in sieges that could have ended. Justice matters, but so does the credibility of the mechanism that makes surrender possible.

### ARGUMENT B — ARREST THE COMMANDER

A promise made without material facts should not automatically become immunity for grave acts. Safe conduct can end a siege without erasing responsibility discovered afterward. If negotiated protection always outranks accountability, the worst offenders gain a route to protection precisely when their crimes are still unknown. Peace loses legitimacy when the price of keeping one word is denying consequences to those who were never part of the bargain.

**Shared question:** Is the greater responsibility to preserve trust in future promises — or to prevent a promise from protecting wrongdoing it was never meant to cover?
