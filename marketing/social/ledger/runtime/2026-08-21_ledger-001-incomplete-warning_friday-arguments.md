## The two arguments used

### PUBLISH NOW — agency under uncertainty

People cannot protect themselves from a danger they are not told about. If the warning is credible, withholding it transfers the risk from the public to the institution and denies people the chance to decide how much uncertainty they are willing to live with.

### WAIT FOR PROOF — responsibility for secondary harm

Information can cause irreversible harm before its truth is settled. If incomplete evidence can trigger panic, displacement, violence or other avoidable deaths, responsibility includes testing the warning before releasing it rather than treating disclosure itself as morally neutral.

The positions share the same stated objective: protect lives. They disagree about who should carry uncertainty and who is responsible for harms caused by action versus delay.

# CAPTION — TikTok / Instagram Reels / YouTube Shorts

The interesting split is not truth versus lies.

Both sides believe they are protecting people.

One argument says a credible warning belongs to the people who must live with the danger. Keeping it back denies them the chance to act on uncertainty themselves.

The other says releasing incomplete evidence can cause real and irreversible harm. Responsibility does not end when information leaves your hands.

So the disagreement is deeper:

**Who should carry uncertainty — the public, or the institution holding the warning?**

There is no official correct answer.
