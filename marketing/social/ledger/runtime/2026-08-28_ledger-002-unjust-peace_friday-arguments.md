## Live dilemma

A peace treaty would end a war and likely save thousands of lives, but one clause leaves a small border community under the rule of the side that abused them. Rejecting the treaty may restart the war.

## The two arguments

### SIGN THE PEACE — responsibility for the lives you can save now

Refusing a flawed peace does not restore the border community. It risks adding thousands more dead to an injustice that already exists. A treaty can be morally compromised and still be the least harmful available action; peace may also create the first realistic chance to change the border without another war.

### REFUSE THE PEACE — some people cannot become the price of everyone else's safety

If peace is purchased by knowingly leaving a vulnerable community with those who abused them, the treaty does more than tolerate an injustice: it teaches every future negotiator whose safety can be traded when the numbers are large enough. Ending a war matters, but so does refusing to make abandonment an accepted instrument of peace.
