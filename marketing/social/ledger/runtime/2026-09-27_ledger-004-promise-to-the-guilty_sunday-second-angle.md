## Sunday second-angle assumption

Change one consequence:

**The commander offers to give you evidence that could identify other responsible people and help victims establish what happened — but only if the promised safe conduct is honored.**

That does not remove the original dilemma. It changes what each choice may cost.

- **Honor the promise:** preserve trust in surrender terms and possibly gain evidence against more people, but allow someone credibly accused of grave acts to leave under your protection.
- **Arrest the commander:** refuse to let safe conduct become protection from accountability, but risk losing testimony and evidence that could expose a wider chain of responsibility.

Neither option is framed as the official answer.

## Community prompt

The changed assumption is not meant to solve the dilemma. It changes the distribution of consequences.

**Decision-threshold question:**

> What single fact about the commander's evidence, the seriousness of his own responsibility, or the effect on future surrender terms would make you switch sides?

Useful follow-ups:
- If his evidence would convict ten other people, does that change your answer?
- If the evidence could be obtained without him, does the promise become less important?
- If safe conduct explicitly guaranteed departure but said nothing about later prosecution, where would you draw the line?
- Does the number of future lives potentially saved by credible surrender terms ever outweigh immediate accountability?
- Is cooperation after surrender a reason to keep the promise, or an attempt to bargain away responsibility?
