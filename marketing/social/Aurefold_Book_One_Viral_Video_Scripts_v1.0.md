# Aurefold — Book One Viral Short-Form Video Scripts v1.0

**Purpose:** Fan acquisition for TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and Instagram Reels.

**Source:** *The Bell of Silence*, English Master v1.7.

**Status:** CREATIVE MARKETING WORKING MASTER — non-canonical promotional material. These scripts do not create or amend canon.

**Format direction:** Vertical 9:16, social-first pacing, captions, human conflict before lore, no objective confirmation of magic.

---

## SCRIPT 1 — THE GIRL WHO REFUSED TO LIE

### Core angle
Sela as the emotional acquisition hook: everyone wants certainty from her, while her most dangerous act is refusing to pretend she has it.

### Narration

> **“Everyone wants her to say she heard a god.”**
>
> Sela heard something in the mountains.
>
> Maybe a voice.
> Maybe wind.
> Maybe nothing.
>
> But House Whitehart has an entire institution built to decide what people like her are allowed to have heard.
>
> They test her.
> They question her.
> They turn her silence into evidence.
>
> And Sela keeps giving them the most dangerous answer possible:
>
> **“I don’t know.”**
>
> Because in a world built on certainty…
> an honest doubt can become an act of rebellion.
>
> **THE BELL OF SILENCE**
> Book One of **AUREFOLD**
>
> **Read it free. Decide for yourself.**

### Production through-line
A coarse strip of work cloth turning through Sela’s fingers, tightening as pressure builds, then stopping.

## SCRIPT 2 — NINE DEAD. TWELVE MISSING.

### Core angle
The gate catastrophe becomes history in real time. The hook is not simply violence; it is that two defensible records refuse to become one truth.

### Narration

> **“Nine bodies.”**
>
> **“Twelve missing.”**
>
> Same gate.
> Same morning.
> Same disaster.
>
> The crowd pushes uphill.
> The gate opens inward.
> Someone screams to open it.
> Someone else screams to hold.
>
> Fifteen minutes later, people are dead.
>
> Whitehart counts **nine bodies**.
>
> The valley counts **twelve missing**.
>
> They count again.
>
> **Nine.**
> **Twelve.**
>
> Tomas is asked which number is true.
>
> He writes down both.
>
> Because sometimes history does not begin with the truth.
>
> It begins with **two records that refuse to agree.**
>
> **AUREFOLD — THE BELL OF SILENCE**
>
> **Whose history would you believe?**

### Production through-line
Two wooden count boards remain physically separate throughout the film and are never visually reconciled.

## SCRIPT 3 — THE BLANK PAGE

### Core angle
One object, one impossible decision: Tomas must either give the institution certainty it does not possess or refuse to use the power entrusted to him.

### Narration

> **“He can write one of two things.”**
>
> If Tomas writes in black…
> the book will declare Sela **true**.
>
> Every denial she makes after that becomes humility.
> Every mistake becomes something believers can explain away.
>
> If he writes in red…
> he condemns her.
>
> But he does not have the evidence.
>
> So the institution tells him:
> **choose.**
>
> Tomas lifts the pen.
>
> His hand shakes.
>
> And then he makes the one decision the system cannot use.
>
> He puts the pen down.
>
> **The page stays blank.**
>
> It costs him his office.
>
> And Sela is still not an answer.
>
> **THE BELL OF SILENCE**
>
> **What would you have written?**

### Production through-line
A dark ink pen over a ruled Ledger page. The pen approaches the page repeatedly but the page remains blank through the final shot.

## CAMPAIGN CREATIVE LOCKS

- Primary objective: build Aurefold fandom, not simply advertise a book.
- Human dilemma before world exposition.
- No lore dump about the Great Houses in the opening hook.
- No objective statement that Sela heard a god, magic, prophecy, or supernatural voice.
- The gate opens inward.
- Nine bodies and twelve missing remain separate counts.
- No definitive first aggressor at the gate.
- Sela’s Ledger page remains blank.
- Visual identity: mature editorial motion graphics; parchment, charcoal, weathered stone, soot black, restrained ember-rust accent.
- Avoid generic glossy AI-fantasy trailer language and imagery.
- CTA should invite judgment, interpretation, or reading rather than merely say “buy the book.”
