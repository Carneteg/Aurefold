# Aurefold — Book One Viral Short Scripts v1.0

**Status:** MARKETING / SOCIAL VIDEO MATERIAL — NOT CANON

**Source basis:** *Aurefold: The Bell of Silence* — English Master v1.7, Adopted Governing Master.

**Campaign goal:** Build fandom and reader curiosity for Aurefold through three distinct short-form social videos for TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and Instagram Reels.

## Locked production direction

- Language: English
- Format: 9:16 vertical
- Target length: 60 seconds per film
- Visual direction: Editorial Motion Graphics
- Campaign visual DNA: mature parchment-and-charcoal editorial collage; warm parchment neutrals, soot black, weathered stone grey, one ember-rust accent
- Narration: one consistent narrator across all three films
- Subtitles: burned-in captions intended
- Creative rule: human conflict before lore; no generic epic-fantasy exposition; no objective confirmation of magic
- Acquisition strategy: each film should work as a standalone micro-story rather than as a conventional book trailer

---

# FILM 1 — THE GIRL WHO REFUSED TO LIE

## Core purpose

Build attachment to Sela and introduce Aurefold's central tension between certainty, belief, institutions, and honest doubt.

## Final approved script

> **“Everyone wants her to say she heard a god.”**
>
> Sela heard something in the mountains.
>
> Maybe a voice.
> Maybe wind.
> Maybe nothing.
>
> But House Whitehart has an entire institution built to decide what people like her are allowed to have heard.
>
> They test her.
> They question her.
> They turn her silence into evidence.
>
> And Sela keeps giving them the most dangerous answer possible:
>
> **“I don’t know.”**
>
> Because in a world built on certainty…
> an honest doubt can become an act of rebellion.
>
> **THE BELL OF SILENCE**
> Book One of **AUREFOLD**
>
> **Read it free. Decide for yourself.**

## Through-line

**Physical object:** Sela's work cloth.

The cloth begins as an ordinary working object, tightens as the questioning becomes more invasive, and becomes still when the trial reaches the question Sela refuses to answer falsely.

## Creative note

The film must sell Sela before it sells Aurefold. The supernatural ambiguity must remain intact: voice, wind, or nothing are all viable interpretations.

# FILM 2 — NINE DEAD. TWELVE MISSING.

## Core purpose

Create the strongest immediate social-media hook while showing how Aurefold turns catastrophe into competing history rather than clean truth.

## Final approved script

> **“Nine bodies.”**
>
> **“Twelve missing.”**
>
> Same gate.
> Same morning.
> Same disaster.
>
> The crowd pushes uphill.
> The gate opens inward.
> Someone screams to open it.
> Someone else screams to hold.
>
> Fifteen minutes later, people are dead.
>
> Whitehart counts **nine bodies**.
>
> The valley counts **twelve missing**.
>
> They count again.
>
> **Nine.**
> **Twelve.**
>
> Tomas is asked which number is true.
>
> He writes down both.
>
> Because sometimes history does not begin with the truth.
>
> It begins with **two records that refuse to agree.**
>
> **AUREFOLD — THE BELL OF SILENCE**
>
> **Whose history would you believe?**

## Through-line

**Physical object:** The two count boards.

The boards begin as administrative tools, acquire moral weight as the casualties are counted, and end side by side with neither one allowed to erase the other.

## Creative note

Do not identify a first aggressor. Do not reconcile nine bodies with twelve missing. The horror is not only the crush; it is the immediate creation of two supportable but incompatible histories.

# FILM 3 — THE BLANK PAGE

## Core purpose

Turn Tomas's institutional dilemma into a simple, morally participatory visual story that invites comments and debate.

## Final approved script

> **“He can write one of two things.”**
>
> If Tomas writes in black…
> the book will declare Sela **true**.
>
> Every denial she makes after that becomes humility.
> Every mistake becomes something believers can explain away.
>
> If he writes in red…
> he condemns her.
>
> But he does not have the evidence.
>
> So the institution tells him:
> **choose.**
>
> Tomas lifts the pen.
>
> His hand shakes.
>
> And then he makes the one decision the system cannot use.
>
> He puts the pen down.
>
> **The page stays blank.**
>
> It costs him his office.
>
> And Sela is still not an answer.
>
> **THE BELL OF SILENCE**
>
> **What would you have written?**

## Through-line

**Physical object:** The pen and blank Ledger page.

The pen moves closer to the page as the institutional pressure rises, hovers over the ruled space at the decision point, and finally lies across the untouched lines.

## Creative note

The blank page is not presented as easy liberation or moral victory. Tomas refuses two dishonest verdicts, loses his office, and leaves Sela unresolved.

# Campaign hierarchy

1. **NINE DEAD. TWELVE MISSING.** — strongest acquisition / scroll-stop concept.
2. **THE BLANK PAGE** — strongest discussion / comment / moral-debate concept.
3. **THE GIRL WHO REFUSED TO LIE** — strongest Sela attachment / protagonist-fandom concept.

Together the three films should establish an Aurefold social identity built around unresolved human choices, competing histories, institutional pressure, and memorable characters rather than lore catalogues or generic fantasy spectacle.
