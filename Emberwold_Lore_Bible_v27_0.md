# PART 0 — THE STORY'S DNA

This part is fixed. Everything written in Emberwold must serve it.

## Core form

- **Genre and tone:** low-key epic fantasy in **mystic realism**.
- **Magic:** nearly invisible, never reliably repeatable, always open to mundane and supernatural readings.
- **Central theme:** competing philosophies of power and leadership, not a simple contest between good and evil.
- **Reader's governing question:** **Which house would I follow?**
- **Dramatic emphasis:** intimate choices create public disasters. Battles, migrations, persecutions, famines, and dynastic changes are consequences of personal acts.
- **Structure:** a long-running series. No final closure is promised. Characters die, institutions outlive them, new people inherit old damage.
- **Moral grayscale:** each of the Ten has produced real good and real victims. A house must be defensible by an intelligent, decent person on the page.
- **Historical method:** the past is treated as something that happened. Sources disagree, records are partial, and official memory has motives.
- **The central mystery:** a forgotten eleventh house was erased from history. It is never named or proven with certainty.

## Series tagline

> **The map ends where the truth begins.**

This is a series-level phrase and the motto attached to Plate I. It may recur in-world, but repetition must be controlled. The Book One ending — *The map ended. She kept walking.* — remains the definitive dramatic use.

## The moral floor

The Ten Great Houses represent competing philosophies. Each has created good, each carries guilt, and none is the singular evil of the world.

**Ashen Crown stands outside this equivalence.** Its people remain human and morally varied. Its ideology of purification, classification, dehumanization, and extermination is not treated as another neutral leadership philosophy.

> A philosophy becomes most dangerous when it gives people permission to stop seeing other people as people.

## Historical shadows

The hidden historical inspiration behind a house shapes both its achievement and its darkness:

- its victories;
- its methods of coercion;
- the people it excludes;
- the victims its philosophy produces;
- the propaganda that cleans the memory afterward.

A major historical atrocity becomes canon only after the gate has approved:

1. its in-world name;
2. the perpetrator;
3. a named victim;
4. a survivor account;
5. the official version;
6. the present-day consequence.

Sexual coercion is never eroticized or graphically depicted. It is shown through loss of choice, dependence, debt, threat, aftermath, and respectable language.

| House | Philosophy | Hidden model |

| Blackthorn | Intellect | Hannibal |
| Ashbourne | Courage | Alexander |

The models remain hidden. No recognizable names, ethnic registers, heraldry, quotations, uniforms, or biographical copies may expose them.

## Essential distinctions

- **Blackthorn vs Ravenshade:** Blackthorn plans; Ravenshade knows.
- **Blackcrest vs Tidebreaker:** Blackcrest decides what the realm remembers; Tidebreaker decides what knowledge the realm is permitted to receive.
- **Ashbourne vs Stormrider:** Ashbourne worships the individual leap; Stormrider mistrusts the individual who rides apart.
- **Ironvale vs Phoenix:** Ironvale improves what exists; Phoenix clears the existing world for what comes next.
- **Whitehart vs Blackcrest:** Whitehart gives authority to the unheard dead; Blackcrest gives authority to the written version.

# PART 1 — FOUNDATIONS OF HISTORY

## Depth of time

Known political history is shallow but dense: roughly **three to five centuries**. The founding generation remains close enough for letters, weapons, ruins, family names, and institutional habits to survive.

The erasure of the forgotten eleventh is therefore not prehistoric myth. It is a fresh absence inside an otherwise documented age.

## Founding event

The Ten arose from a shared conquest and civil war, officially remembered as the **War of the Unification**.

## The eleventh's official role

The sanctioned account casts the missing party as traitor, cause, or necessary sacrifice. This judgment is not treated as trustworthy.

# PART 2 — THE TIMELINE

Years are counted in **A.U. — After the Unification**.

The series begins in **396 A.U.**, four years before the 400-year Jubilee.

| Year | Event | Canonical meaning |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | War of the Unification; Ash Oath | The Ten are founded from a victory officially described as concord. |
| 3–4 | The Silent Year | An unnatural absence in the records; likely connected to the erasure, never proven. |
| c. 40–70 | Sundering of the Ten Philosophies | The founders die; their descendants convert survival methods into doctrines. |
| c. 120–150 | The First Betrayal | The postwar order proves fragile; whispers of an excluded party return. |
| c. 200–250 | Age of Chronicles | Histories become institutions. Blackcrest's authority hardens. |
| 396 | Book One begins | Whitehart's local crisis unfolds while Garron's failing destabilizes the wider realm. |
| 400 | The Jubilee | Future series clock. The official retelling and the buried truth move toward collision. |

## The war's three names

- **Official:** *The War of the Unification* / *The Great Unification*.
- **Popular:** *The War of the Eleven Banners*.
- **Suppressed:** *The Divided Fire*.

## The founding contradiction

The Chronicle's authorized prologue states that:

- **eleven lords** gathered;
- **ten customs** were named;
- **the ten Great Houses** pressed their seals;
- the order is described as though it had always been ten.

The discrepancy is deliberate and unresolved.

# PART 3 — THE SOURCES

No source is definitive. Truth lives in the disagreement between them.

## The Chronicle of Victory

The authorized record, maintained through Blackcrest. Coherent, beautiful, politically useful, and unreliable where legitimacy is at stake.

It preserves the contradiction of eleven lords and ten houses without acknowledging that a contradiction exists.

## The Ash Fragments

Burned and incomplete dissenting texts of uncertain authenticity. They describe civil war rather than defense against an external enemy and sometimes portray the forgotten party as attempting to stop the slaughter.

## The Songs

Oral tradition, work songs, children's counting rhymes, burial verses, road songs, market insults. They preserve errors the official apparatus could not fully police.

## The Marginal Notes

Questions, calculations, objections, and names written beside official records. Several historians associated with them disappeared or ceased writing.

## Ordinary institutional silence

Not every missing record points to the forgotten eleventh.

Whitehart deliberately destroys trial notes. Mother Alaine authored the rule after she was raised as Mother. The stated doctrine is that judgment must live in the judge rather than the page. The private reason is that pages can be compared, and comparison might expose the seams in her fifty-year performance.

This is **ghost-free**: a human hiding a human lie.

# PART 4 — THE FORGOTTEN ELEVENTH

## Locked rule

> The forgotten eleventh shall never be named with certainty, and every proposed truth about it must come from an unreliable source.

No fixed name, confirmed philosophy, confirmed seat, confirmed descendants, or complete history may be established.

## Working hypothesis — not truth

The eleventh may have rejected the premise that power should be divided among hereditary houses. Its betrayal may have been refusal to accept the Ash Oath itself.

This remains a hypothesis, not canon fact.

## Two explanations for the erasure

Both must remain viable.

### Human explanation

- coordinated destruction of records;
- forbidden names;
- scraped seals;
- redistributed children;
- three or four generations of enforced silence;
- later descendants inheriting the silence without knowing its origin.

### Mystical explanation

- the name cannot be retained;
- pages fail at the same point;
- people forget when they try to remember;
- those who dig too deeply become difficult to remember themselves.

The series never confirms either explanation.

## Roads to the ghost

### Close roads

- **Whitehart listens.** The erased dead should be the loudest under Whitehart theology.
- **Stormrider did.** The house may have been the unwitting hand of the erasure.
- **Blackcrest wrote and hid.** Its ancestors authored the useful version and later forgot what they concealed.
- **Stonebear swore.** The Wall of Shame carries a nameless oldest line.
- **Tidebreaker recorded.** The Deep Archive may contain maps or accounts withheld from the realm.
- **Phoenix burned.** The house may stand on or emerge from the erasure's ash.

### Faint roads

- **Blackthorn reads:** one impossible defeat in Halden's notes.
- **Ravenshade hears:** one unplaced debt in old ciphers.
- **Ashbourne rides toward:** an ancestor returned changed from the east.

### No road by design

- **Ironvale searched and found nothing.** The result is honest. It is not a hidden clue.

## Frozen charged elevens

The charged eleven-instances are finite:

- the War of the Eleven Banners;
- the eleven lords in the authorized prologue;
- the Ash Oath's disputed seal count;
- the children's counting rhyme;
- Elvestad's sound;
- the eleventh Shard;
- the possible eleventh strand in the Weaving;
- Bryn Corr's deliberate eleven-to-nine miscount in Book One.

No new eleven motif enters canon without explicit author approval.

# PART 5 — GEOGRAPHY

## The world

The world is **Emberwold**.

The known continent is the **Banner-lands**, named for the War of the Eleven Banners.

Its broad shape is kidney-like, with:

- the **Sleeping Sea** to the west and south;
- the **Silent Mountains** along the east, bending around the northern rim into the northwest;
- the **Ash Fields** at the political center;
- the **Shards** scattered in the southern sea.

## The Sleeping Sea

Navigable near the coast, fog-bound and unreliable farther out. Ships that return from the deep sea bring contradictory accounts.

## The Silent Mountains

One continuous mountain system, not several separate ranges.

- Ashbourne holds the eastern edge at Dawnwatch.
- Stonebear holds the northeastern foot and the roads.
- Whitehart inhabits the northwestern bend in the Light Heights.

## The Ash Fields

The densely inhabited central plain where the decisive battle was fought and the Ash Oath sworn.

No Great House owns the center outright. The void itself is politically meaningful.

## The seat of power — three names

No name is confirmed as the original.

- **Ederhamn:** official, “the oath-haven”; strange because the city is inland.
- **Elvestad:** popular; officially glossed as river-town, though no major river justifies it; the sound carries the eleven-riddle.
- **Ederstad:** suppressed, older spelling found in fragments.

These names are never translated.

## Map convention

Rivers and lakes remain unnamed until a scene requires a name. Each naming is a canon decision.

# PART 6 — MAGIC'S HIDDEN RULEBOOK

None of these rules is explained to the reader.

## The five principles

1. **Double explanation:** every potentially supernatural event must sustain a mundane and a supernatural reading.
2. **Never repeatable:** if it can be reproduced reliably, it is technique, not magic.
3. **Never a dependable tool:** no one commands magic on demand.
4. **The measurable effect is social:** belief changes what people do; that consequence is real even when the miracle is not.
5. **No secret final system:** the ambiguity is not hiding a neat mechanism. The ambiguity is the truth.

## Craft rules

- Magic lives in timing more than broken physics.
- The witness always wants something.
- Seeking proof creates a new question.
- Great historical effects arise from small visible traces.
- Borderline events remain rare.
- Taboo events connected to the erasure are rarer still.

## House attitudes

| House | Attitude toward the unseen |
|---|---|
| Whitehart | Embraces and institutionalizes it |
| Ravenshade | Exploits belief as information and leverage |
| Blackcrest | Selects which signs are remembered |
| Tidebreaker | Collects and investigates anomalies |
| Blackthorn | Publicly dismisses, privately models belief |
| Phoenix | Treats superstition as a problem to replace with construction |
| Stonebear | Respects signs but rarely interprets them publicly |
| Stormrider | Uses shared signs to reinforce cohesion |
| Ashbourne | Defies omens as a test of courage |
| Ironvale | Attempts measurement and replication; always fails at certainty |

## 7.1 HOUSE BLACKTHORN — INTELLECT

**Seat:** Thorn Hall, Thornland.  
**Sigil:** thorn branch snaring a sword.  
**House word:** *We see the winter in the summer.*

### Philosophy

The battle is won before it is fought. Planning, patience, contradiction, and strategic foresight outrank force.

### Gift

Blackthorn sees consequences before others do and can defeat stronger enemies through design.

### Hidden tragedy

It can win every engagement and still fail to end the war. Understanding becomes paralysis.

### Culture

- Ritual: **The Objection**, where every plan must be attacked.
- Shame: being surprised.
- Seat features: Game Hall, Defeat Library, deceptive architecture.
- Social cost: family affection is often expressed as correction.

### Key figures

- **Alder Blackthorn**, head, brilliant and paralyzed by foresight.
- **Wren Blackthorn**, heir, terrified of becoming her father; acts before certainty.
- **Corin Blackthorn**, gifted mind without appetite for rule.
- **Maeve Torn**, loyal to the house more than any individual Blackthorn.
- **Jorem Blackthorn**, potential betrayer who sees submission to a stronger power as realism.
- **Halden**, vanished archivist whose notes contain an impossible defeat.

### Ghost relation

Faint road only. Halden's impossible defeat is one anomaly, not the house's central drama.

### Writing guard

Blackthorn plans from what an enemy ought to do. Do not let it become Ravenshade with maps.

## 7.2 HOUSE RAVENSHADE — INFORMATION

**Seat:** Duskport, Sleep Coast.  
**Sigil:** raven against a setting sun, one eye in shadow.  
**House word:** *We hear what the silence says.*

### Philosophy

The one who knows most needs least. Influence comes through secrets, debts, timing, and indispensability.

### Gift

Ravenshade can move larger powers without appearing to command them.

### Hidden tragedy

Its power is borrowed. The stronger ally may withdraw the shoulder at any time.

### Culture

- Ritual: **The Banquet**, feast and information market.
- No throne room; power lives in parlors, letters, ciphers, and debt ledgers.
- Duskport itself functions as the house.
- Affection and usefulness are difficult to separate.

### Present crisis

**The Patron Crisis.** Ravenshade's long-standing patron is failing. The patron's identity is deliberately unnamed until a future book requires the choice.

### Key figures

- **Sabra Ravenshade**, secure, skilled, without self-doubt; structural danger rather than anxious intrigue.
- **Emeric**, consort, the one relationship Sabra permits to remain unsolved.
- **Lyra**, heir and envoy; ghost-free; intimacy under instrumental conditions.
- **Old Corvin**, archivist loyal to the archive, keeper of the unplaced debt.

### Ghost relation

Faint road only: one debt whose owner cannot be identified.

### Writing guard

Ravenshade knows what is happening but may not know what should be done. Do not turn it into Blackthorn's planning culture.

## 7.3 HOUSE ASHBOURNE — COURAGE

**Seat:** Dawnwatch, Edgelands.  
**Sigil:** leaping stag or hind over an abyss.  
**House word:** *We land where we dare.*

### Philosophy

Hesitation is the only true defeat. Momentum and visible courage create authority.

### Gift

Ashbourne can move when every other house is still arguing.

### Hidden tragedy

It cannot stop. Victory creates the need for the next advance. The house spends its young to preserve the meaning of earlier deaths.

### Culture

- Ritual: **The Leap**.
- Wall of Names for those killed in Leaps and frontier actions.
- Dawnwatch is built to ride out, not endure siege.
- Scars are public arguments.

### Key figures

- **Roderick**, head, once the ideal rider, now secretly doubts the house.
- **Kael**, heir, warm and infectious certainty; movement as refusal to grieve.
- **Isolde**, sharper and more cautious, passed over.
- **Bran the Unfallen**, weapon-master who teaches the young and watches their names enter the wall.

### Ghost relation

Faint road through an ancestor who crossed east and returned changed.

### Writing guard

Ashbourne's courage must create real achievements. Its victims do not make its followers foolish.

## 7.4 HOUSE WHITEHART — FAITH

**Seat:** the Bell of Silence, Light Heights, northwestern bend of the Silent Mountains.  
**Sigil:** white hart in profile, head lowered to listen.  
**House word:** **Icke jag** — “not I.”

### Philosophy

Authority does not originate in the leader. It passes through a vessel who has surrendered the self.

The dead are not gone, only silent. The living must learn to listen.

### Gift

Whitehart permits authority to rise from outside bloodline and rank. A shepherd, servant, or forgotten child may become the center of the house if believed Called.

### Hidden tragedy

The Called serves, wins, and is consumed.

If the voice is real, Whitehart repeatedly destroys the sacred vessel it serves. If the voice is not real, it destroys people for believing too deeply. The house can never distinguish the two.

### Core culture

- The living are stewards, never owners.
- Decisions are voiced passively: “it was decided.”
- Children learn the dead's names and sit before the griffes.
- The Called is chosen by perceived hearing, not blood.
- The Tester attempts to distinguish true calling from fear, fraud, sickness, and conviction.
- Whitehart's language of humility can also conceal responsibility.

### The griffes

Open niches for the dead, facing the valley. The wall is both cemetery, archive, congregation, and political audience.

### The burn-rule

Trial working notes are burned the same night.

- Public doctrine: judgment must live in the judge, not the page.
- Private origin: Mother Alaine writes the rule after she is raised as Mother, to prevent future readers from comparing testimony and discovering her fraud.
- Tomas believes the rule ancient.

### The Ledger of the Judged

Three outcomes exist:

1. **Black entry — true:** the person is accepted as genuinely Called.
2. **Red entry — false:** the person is Given Back.
3. **Unclosed page — uncertainty:** no verdict is entered; the person becomes Unwritten.

A sealed page cannot later be completed. Any new decision requires an entirely new testing.

### The Giving-Back

A judged-false hearer is walked above the high line at first snow with bread, cloak, and prayers. No one binds or strikes them. The house descends; they do not.

Some may die. Some may walk east. The uncertainty is both the house's mercy and its cruelty. Whitehart must never be reduced to deliberate executioners.

### The Unwritten

The Unwritten live but lose institutional personhood.

They may work and be fed. Within the house's formal voice they cannot fully:

- testify;
- inherit;
- marry through the recorded rite;
- stand as named witness at a naming;
- receive attribution for labor;
- be addressed in the offices that require an entered identity.

The institution calls this mercy because uncertainty does not kill. The cost is social existence.

### The Winter of the Three Voices

Mother Ilse, Brother Roche, and the creation of the third case are canon. Roche refuses to kill where certainty fails. The institution honors the mercy and inherits its hidden violence.

### Key Book One figures

- **Sela**, 19, goatherd and Called; primary POV.
- **Brother Tomas**, 45; sixteen years a Tester, nine as the Tester.
- **Mother Alaine**, about 60; fifty years of authority built on never hearing.
- **Fen**, 24; tested at 19; five years Unwritten; niche-tender.
- **Sister Wilda**, keeper of law and the woman who let Col go.
- **Brother Osric**, quick, ambitious, capable, and sincere.
- **Steward Ansel**, quantity, order, survival, and unspoken redistribution.
- **Ivet**, 16, loving believer who converts ordinary acts into confirmation.
- **Merta**, mother of Col, moral witness from below the boundary.

### Sela — locked facts

- Prologue: first on-page hearing, no direction.
- Trial: first witnessed eastward turn, doubled by wind and group suggestion.
- She never receives proof that the voice exists.
- Her authority grows most when she denies authority.
- She speaks Col's name.
- She warns Harl about wet ground without claiming revelation.
- She walks east at the end of Book One.
- Harl's knife travels with her.

### Tomas — locked facts

- Reads hands better than faces.
- Burns notes as office discipline.
- Carries Col's red verdict.
- Begins to question the system rather than merely individual candidates.
- Offers retesting but cannot reopen sealed pages.
- Ends Book One with his office altered but not abolished.

### Alaine — locked facts

- Has never heard the dead.
- Uses borrowed descriptions to perform hearing.
- Authored the burn-rule.
- Her chair and stillness become architecture of authority.
- Receives exactly three interludes in Book One.
- Does not confess.
- Lets Sela leave without clarifying whether the act is mercy, fear, love, or preservation.

### Fen — locked facts

- Fourth POV, opening in Movement II with an excuse he tells himself.
- Knows the wall of names better than the recognized members.
- Lost a possible marriage to Nessa because he cannot be entered.
- Sells information to Perrin.
- Does not tell Sela that he sold her fear.
- Requests that his name be used.
- Remains at Whitehart after Sela leaves.

### Merta, Col, and Wilda

- Col was tested false sixteen years before Book One.
- Merta keeps a first-snow vigil below the boundary with his coat.
- Wilda walked him up.
- Wilda later reveals that she let him go.
- Col's fate remains unresolved.
- Merta receives possibility, not closure.

### The maintained grave

Canon package:

- one body;
- adult;
- old burial;
- face-down;
- hands absent from the grave;
- fine, tightly woven, high-status wool unfamiliar to the Light Heights;
- outside the consecrated line;
- no name, count, seal, inscription, or text;
- stones re-laid by different hands across generations;
- drainage maintained;
- Alaine orders resealing and a vigil until the last stone is laid.

All explanations remain open:

- oath-breaker custom;
- plague or crime reburial;
- extinct penitential rite;
- later planted grave;
- mistaken dating;
- ordinary guilt maintained across families.

The grave does not prove the forgotten eleventh.

### Whitehart ghost relation

Close road. The theology makes forgotten dead structurally important, but Book One proves nothing.

## 7.5 HOUSE STORMRIDER — UNITY

**Seat:** the Hub, northern rim of the Ash Fields.  
**Sigil:** interwoven reins / braided line binding ten strands.  
**House word:** *Many horses, one hoof.*

### Philosophy

Division is the only enemy. Cohesion is power.

### Gift

Stormrider ends feuds, joins clans, standardizes justice, opens routes, and prevents cycles of revenge.

### Hidden tragedy

Unity resides in the uniter. The heirs inherit the realm but not the glue. The greater the binding, the bloodier the possible collapse.

### Culture

- children raised in mixed packs;
- separation is the deepest punishment;
- leadership based on ability to bind rather than simple birthright;
- belonging is medicine and coercion at once.

### Key figures

- **Garron, the Binder**, about 64, central uniter.
- **Una, Orrin, Katla**, canon names in the succession field; exact future configuration remains open.
- **Kavan**, canon world figure: socially fluent, useful, cruel through controlled humiliation and forced reintegration.
- **Ryn**, familial/paternal bond with Kavan; never sexualized or rewritten as romance.

### Garron's irreversible choice

Garron knows what Kavan is and continues to use and protect him because Kavan's methods hold regions together that might otherwise return to war.

The question remains:

> Did Garron create peace, or make resistance too terrifying to imagine?

### Book One seed

- **Roan**, 29, carries contradictory reports of Garron's death.
- **Ottar**, 38, carries unexplained signs of Kavan's work: separate service, difficulty using *I*, and a mended horsehair braid.
- Garron never appears on-page in Book One.
- His death arrives as distant weather and becomes politically real only at the end.

### Ghost relation

Close road. Stormrider may have been the erasure's unwitting hand. Nothing is proved.

### Writing guard

Stormrider must visibly keep peace. Its coercion cannot be shown without the peace that intelligent followers believe it preserves.

## 7.6 HOUSE IRONVALE — INNOVATION

**Seat:** Forge Gap, Ore Valleys.  
**Sigil:** anvil wrapped in flame, crossed by hammer and tongs.  
**House word:** *What we cannot rebuild owns us.*

### Philosophy

The world is unfinished. Improvement is duty.

### Gift

Roads, bridges, tools, canals, harvest systems, steel, and works that materially improve life.

### Hidden tragedy

The first generation pays the test cost of every innovation. Risk becomes clean when priced and signed.

### Culture

- workshop floor instead of throne room;
- Drafting Hall as treasury;
- proving contracts;
- strong trade that spreads inventions and erodes advantage.

### Key figures

- **Master Halvard**, ~58, measures the inexplicable and counts when shaken.

## 7.7 HOUSE BLACKCREST — THE VICTOR'S HISTORY

**Seat:** Crown-watch, edge of the Ash Fields.  
**Sigil:** open book, one page ink-dark or blank; quill that also reads as sword.  
**House words:** public motto: *The sword wins the day. The pen wins every day to come.* Portrait word: *He who writes, remembers.*

### Philosophy

What survives in the record becomes what happened.

### Gift

Blackcrest creates shared versions that prevent renewed violence, settle inheritance, preserve legitimacy, and give grief a usable shape.

### Hidden tragedy

The house wrote useful lies for so long that it no longer knows what the first hidden truth was.

### Culture

- Permanent Book / Chronicle of Victory;
- Ash Urns containing burned alternatives;
- Clean Page doctrine;
- administrative power rather than battlefield power;
- dependence of every house on its favorable memory.

### Key figures

- **Chronicler Aldous**, ~61, head, guardian of a void.
- **Vaela**, heir, future pressure toward opening the house's own hidden pages.
- **Perrin**, Jubilee Herald, Book One's Blackcrest agent; charming, useful, morally compromised, never given POV in Book One.

### Harrowick

The Harrowick granary fire and Perrin's grain-barge method are canon. The method saves lives and demonstrates that arranged history can be materially merciful.

### Book One actions

- Perrin gathers testimony for the Jubilee Chronicle.
- He helps prevent deaths on the road.
- He pays for shelter and creates obligations.
- He recognizes Fen as a source and person.
- He uses Fen's information.
- Merta refuses his corrected entry.

### Grayscale guard

Blackcrest must win arguments often enough to hurt. If the reader can dismiss it as the obvious villain, the house has failed.

### Ghost relation

Close road. Blackcrest wrote and hid the erasure, then inherited the absence rather than the truth.

## 7.8 HOUSE STONEBEAR — HONOR

**Seat:** Oath-hold, northeastern foot of the Silent Mountains.  
**Sigil:** standing bear with one paw on an oath-stone.  
**House word:** *A word is the only stone that holds.*

### Philosophy

A promise must remain true when keeping it becomes expensive.

### Gift

Stonebear makes treaties possible. Its word gives value to every other agreement in the realm.

### Hidden tragedy

Literal honor can be weaponized. The house may preserve trust while destroying the people who pay the cost.

### Culture

- Oath-Stone;
- Wall of Shame, including the nameless oldest line;
- Guest Hall without a lock;
- witnessed oaths and guest-right;
- shame displayed rather than hidden.

### Present crisis

The house is being bankrupted by contracts crafted to exploit its literalism.

### Pass Compact

Canon. Stonebear must keep the high road open in every season in return for a fixed toll of thirty marks in old silver, now worth a fraction of its original value.

### Key figures

- **Reverend Torvald**, ~59, at peace with the cost of keeping the word.

### Ghost relation

## 7.10 HOUSE PHOENIX — THE FUTURE

**Sigil:** rising bird, wings half-formed from flame.

Infrastructure, planned growth, clearance of failed systems, higher yields, and willingness to build beyond inherited limits.

# PART 9 — BOOK ONE CANON

## Date and place

### Ledger and Unwritten

Sela learns the black, red, and unclosed outcomes. The blank page has no name. Fen's status becomes legible.

### Col

Merta's first-snow vigil and Sela's speaking of Col's name expose Whitehart's living wound.

### The grave

The maintained grave is opened by the slide. It carries no proof, only pressure.

### Nothing happened

Sela publicly denies revelation. The denial becomes evidence of holiness.

### Perrin arrives

Blackcrest begins recording the valley. Perrin saves lives, forms debts, and discovers Fen.

Fen gives Perrin useful truths. Merta refuses a corrected entry for Col and identifies the violence in “kind” recording.

Sela warns Harl about wet ground. She refuses to claim the dead spoke. Harl takes the neck, the safe route, and dies when the wet spring takes the shelf.

Stonebear's literal law determines compensation. The ruling is honorable and ruinous.

### Perrin leaves

His methods continue after his departure.

# PART 12 — WRITING AND CONTINUITY LAW

## Character law

- Houses carry philosophies; people carry appetites.
- No character is the perfect mouthpiece for a house.
- Change is shown through behavior, not announced.
- Experience transfers between chapters.
- A changed physical tell is a canon event.
- Growth may mean hardening, corruption, or self-deception.
- Supporting characters require wants, work, humor, and blind spots.

## Violence law

- Violence follows a philosophical choice.
- The body and grief are real; meaning remains disputed.
- No victim exists merely to decorate another arc.
- A good person must be able to defend the brutal mechanism.
- Ordinary death outnumbers political death; political death outnumbers mythic death.
- Graphic detail is reduced unless it carries necessary human truth.

## Prose law

- POV controls syntax, metaphor, perception, and ignorance.
- Sela: body, ground, work, weather, animals, exits.
- Tomas: categories, evidence, office, definitions; syntax fails when categories fail.
- Fen: wit as labor; jokes may fail; pain simplifies language.
- Alaine: passive construction, procedure, omission, self-correction; reader understands first.
- Historian voice: sources, gaps, contradictions; never final moral verdict.
- Do not restore the shared paradox-machine.
- Do not explain an action and then explain its meaning again.

## Mystery law

- Never verify the voice.
- Never make the voice repeatable.
- Never confirm the forgotten eleventh.
- Never allow Ashen Crown to become the answer.
- Never let a single clue explain multiple houses unless explicitly gated.
- Ironvale's no-road remains honest.

## Book One lock summary

- 27 numbered chapters.
- Three Alaine interludes.
- Four POVs only.
- Perrin no POV.
- Prologue: eleven lords, ten customs, ten seals/houses.
- Prologue hearing has no direction.
- Trial is first east-turn.
- Harl takes the neck.
- Wet spring is sufficient.
- Nine / twelve gate counts.
- Sela never learns Fen sold her fear.
- Harl's knife goes east.
- Garron stays off-page.
- Ashen Crown appears once.
- Final lines remain unchanged.
