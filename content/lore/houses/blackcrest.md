# HOUSE BLACKCREST: THE VICTOR'S HISTORY

- **Ideal:** History as Governance.
- **The Foundational Question:** *"Who decides which surviving account becomes usable history?"*
- **The Lived Reality:**
  - ***The Good:*** Blackcrest editors value ordinary testimony and human details because official House accounts arrive already polished into sterile symbols. They can preserve and remember people whom power would otherwise completely forget.
  - ***The Victim:*** Blackcrest routinely converts private, painful lives into public political materials. They remove historical uncertainty to create usable law and turn a compassionate sentence into a structural weapon that outlives its author.
- **The Editorial Method:** The Blackcrest central archive works through a strict, multi-stage discipline: **Collection** of mutually incompatible accounts; **Separation** of raw source text from editorial inference; **Comparison** of uncoordinated material details like weather and paths; **Selection** based on political survivability and narrative elegance; and **Attribution** of disagreement when certainty would be false. For Blackcrest, the polished Chronicle is designed to be read, remembered, and acted upon — its elegance is a form of power, not proof of accuracy.

---

### Developer & Canon Guardrails — DO NOT REMOVE

1. Do **not** portray Perrin or Blackcrest heralds as simple, cynical propagandists or manipulative liars. Their kindness and their extraction of history must be simultaneously sincere.
2. The central Blackcrest archive remains highly selective, incomplete, and contested under the canon "every record has an omission" locks. It is not omniscient.
