# HOUSE WHITEHART: THE ARCHITECTURE OF DEVOTION

- **Ideal:** Faith.
- **The Foundational Question:** *"Can a community survive without shared meaning, and what happens when meaning requires certainty?"*
- **The Lived Reality:**
  - ***The Good:*** Whitehart provides profound community cohesion, shared survival resources, and structured sanctuary in a harsh mountain landscape.
  - ***The Victim:*** The **Unwritten** — those ordinary lives and experiences that cannot be cleanly categorized by official procedure, left legally erased and socially invisible.
- **The Liturgical Friction:** In Whitehart, the Tester conducts trials over three nights on thin paper with a stick of charcoal, recording a candidate's hesitation, their breathing, and where their gaze lands before speaking. When the trial is completed, the notes are immediately burned in an iron dish. The official explanation is that the notes are destroyed to prevent a Tester's private uncertainty from hardening into public doctrine. The material reality is that the burning leaves only the supreme authority of the final, entered judgment.

### Developer & Canon Guardrails — DO NOT REMOVE

1. Do **not** write Sela as a chosen savior or confirmed prophet. Frame her as an ordinary goatherd whose practical uncertainty is weaponized.
2. Every apparent miracle or supernatural occurrence (e.g., Sela turning toward the listening arch) must retain a credible non-magical explanation, such as wind, thermal stress, or human expectation.
3. Ensure no high-fantasy tropes (glowing runes, holy magical beams, pure-evil framing) are used in the description.
