Authority status: GOVERNING BOOK-SPECIFIC CONTINUITY. Subordinate to Aurefold Constitution v1.8 and Canon Ledger v1.8. This map does not create Canon Lock #085.
1. Knowledge-control principles
No narrator or character possesses objective proof of magic, the Bell’s cause, the first aggressor at the gate, Col’s final fate, or the erased Eleventh House.
Observation, inference, legal effect, public wording, and later use remain distinguishable even when characters collapse them.
Knowledge may move through speech, records, songs, work, bodily evidence, maps, prices, and omissions; every transfer can alter meaning.
Book One ends with several truths privately held but not institutionally enterable.

Knows her experiences are directions or impressions she cannot verify; knows practical mountain work.
Learns that refusal, silence, and accurate speech can all be converted into authority; chooses to leave.

Fen

Knows records become legal and political material; sees ignored labor and ordinary witnesses.
Learns Whitehart’s blanks are people and that his attention can buy them; leaves systems running.

Long-term pillar; no regular Book Two POV; prior name-erasure wound held for future revelation.

Long-term pillar; carries consequences of violence and inherited work.
Ivet
Novice; Selas intimate mirror
Believes in Sela and in Whitehart forms; also knows goats and shared work.
Learns reverence can wound, apology cannot restore innocence, and love may require letting Sela go.

Merta

Yrsa

Orla
Stonebear witness

Rules closure lawful without calling it right.
Cannot tell Merta what Col knew or repair Whitehart’s past.

Nella
Duskport creditor and organizer
Knows credit, road costs, public prices, and how dependency forms.
Demonstrates grain was dispatched before crisis and offers Sela paid work with explicit refusal.
Cannot make economic power harmless through transparency alone.
Help remains both survival and leverage.
Corrin
Singer and carrier of public wording
Knows songs can preserve and simplify; uses both nine and twelve for meter and meaning.
Is permanently injured by Tam and loses capacity for long verses.
Cannot control quotation once detached from him.

Nessa
Fens lost private life
Knows Fen as a person but must deny him publicly.
Her repeated private warning preserves relationship without restoring social place.
Does not publicly challenge the system.
Remains a living measure of Fens loss.
Col
Absent condemned youth
Known through red entry, Merta, Wilda, route, and contradictions.
No new direct knowledge enters from Col.
Survival and later life unconfirmed.
Protected uncertainty.

Prologue
The Question in the Margin
Wren / external
Whitehart records can be procedurally complete while withholding the evidentiary road to judgment. Perrin receives the Whitehart commission.
Wren identifies the malformed question; Perrin records “Who pays for the blank space?” despite her warning.
Wren and Perrin begin as wary methodological foils.
Jubilee use of Whitehart judgments; authority of Perrin’s eventual record.
1
The Trial of the Goatherd
Tomas / Sela
Sela describes a direction rather than a voice; Tomas and Alaine both turn toward the arch.
Tomas records only observable conduct, then burns the note; he realizes he has tested Sela’s uncertainty but never Alaine’s certainty.
Sela trusts Alaine for practical discipline; Tomas’s confidence in Alaine acquires its first crack.
Whether anything supernatural occurred remains unresolved.
2
The Woman Against the Stone
Alaine
Alaine privately admits she cannot separate any “hearing” from experience, memory, fear, or desire.
The reader learns her authority rests on disciplined ambiguity, accumulated testimony, and institutional adaptation.
Alaine loves Sela and envies her; care and use become inseparable.
Alaine’s deeper private history remains held.
3
The Morning After
Sela
The trial immediately changes food, work, seating, objects, and social distance around Sela.
Ivet’s reverence wounds Sela; Ansel’s practical care also becomes public evidence.
Sela and Ivet begin a damaged but repairable intimacy; Fen is seeded through ordinary work.
No judgment exists, but social classification already functions.
4
The Road Down
Sela / family
The valley economy, Harl’s routes, Tam’s ambitions, and external freight pressures become visible.
Sela sees that her status alters family prices and obligations.
Harl lends but does not give his knife; Sela’s family remains her private scale.
Harl’s knife remains his until later transfer.
5
The Ledger
Sela / Tomas / Fen
Sela learns how black, red, and unclosed entries alter inheritance, office, marriage, and external recognition.
Fen’s page is unfinished; burned reasoning cannot travel with the judgment.
Tomas’s procedural mercy is exposed as a durable wound.
Selas page remains unentered.
6
First Snow
Tomas / Sela / Merta
Sela speaks Col’s name; Tomas repeats it. Merta’s sixteen-year waiting becomes public pressure.
Wilda and Tomas know different parts of Col’s history; Sela does not know whether her impulse meant anything.
Merta’s grief binds Sela, Tomas, and Whitehart to a name the institution cannot resolve.
Col’s survival is unconfirmed.
7
The East Shoulder
Sela / Whitehart
A grave is exposed; age, arrangement, and provenance remain uncertain.
Whitehart, pilgrims, and later investigators acquire mutually incompatible evidentiary interests.
Tam and Sela work together to reclose the ground rather than convert it into proof.
No grave proves Col, magic, or the Eleventh.
8
Nothing Happened
Sela / camp
Sela’s denial becomes a story; fires and pilgrims multiply.
Rumor converts ordinary gestures and omissions into signs.
Sela’s attempts at correction increase her symbolic value.
The source of later false quotations remains distributed.
9
The Man Who Writes It Down
Perrin / Tomas
Perrin arrives under commission and immediately traces labor, custody, prices, and ignored people.
He identifies Fen as a source because others name his function before his person.
Perrin’s kindness and extraction occupy the same relationship.
The future authority of his record remains open.
10
Nobody Ever Asked
Fen
Fen’s life with Nessa and the cost of an unclosed page are revealed.
Perrin’s use of Fen’s name gives him temporary public existence and purchases access.
Fen begins to trust and resent Perrin at once.
Fen’s later system for recognizing labor is future continuity, not resolved here.
11
What the Grave No Longer Shows
Wren / Sela
Wren arrives and reconstructs what the reclosed grave can and cannot support.
Sela refuses to repeat a test; Wren separates observation from inference.
Wren’s method initially feels cleaner than Perrin’s but still turns refusal into evidence.
No objective grave conclusion.
12
The Corrected Entry
Perrin / Wren / Merta
Cols age is corrected while the red judgment stands.
The correction changes future legal and narrative use without repairing the past.
Merta reclaims the good chair; Tomas sees procedure and consequence diverge.
The original basis of Col’s judgment cannot be recovered.
13
Not That Way
Sela / Harl
Early thaw destabilizes roads and transforms Selas practical warning into conflicting public claims.
Harl chooses a route against simplified accounts; deposits and blame attach to Selas words.
Sela and Harl disagree as skilled people, not prophet and follower.
Which account later dominates remains contested.
14
Wet Ground
Sela / Harl / Tam
Harl leaves with nine fares; conditions worsen; Sela attempts to reach him.
Tam, Bryn, and the road community inherit immediate material risk.
Sela acts directly, but cannot control weather, distance, or other adults.
Harl’s survival is not protected.
15
Four Accounts of a Rope
Roderick / Tam / Sela
Four incompatible accounts preserve different pieces of the rescue.
Tam chooses Harl’s older rope for practical reasons; Roderick’s retreat order disappears from the simplified story.
Roderick becomes heroic against his wishes; Tam inherits skill and anger.
No single account owns the rescue.
16
Four Marks a Head
Fen / Perrin
Four bodies arrive; identification, burial, payment, and narrative use diverge.
Fen receives bodies without office; Perrin records a clean sentence that is factually true and morally incomplete.
Harl’s death makes Sela, Tam, Fen, and Perrin’s methods personally costly.
The exact cultural meaning of Harl’s death remains contested.
17
The Only Voice Permitted
Sela / Fen / Alaine
Alaine grants Sela sole permission to speak to Fen.
A seeming right isolates Fen and makes Sela the sanctioned channel.
Sela’s defense becomes another institutionally useful cage; Fen’s anger shifts toward her.
Whitehart does not restore Fen’s standing.
18
What the Notes Said
Alaine
Alaine understands how six words have reorganized the house.
She reads institutional effects while withholding her own uncertainty.
Her love of Sela and preservation of Whitehart become harder to separate.
Alaine’s authority remains intact but morally damaged.
19
The Low Wells
Maera / Tomas / Sela
Lio dies; water counts, illness, marked cups, and closed wells reveal incompatible forms of care.
Maera’s evidence can reduce illness while stigmatizing households; Tomas sees consent fail under emergency.
Sela remains useful but cannot make medicine or administration clean.
White cloth markings persist after illness declines.
20
Four Kinds of Count
Wren / camp
Four counts measure bodies, portions, sleeping places, and represented labor differently.
Nella’s credit and dispatched carts are known but not guaranteed to arrive.
Wren recognizes that later copies may turn one provisional number into population.
No single camp count becomes objective.
21
Water Finds the Missing
Tervan / Fen / Sela
The runnel is built through measurement, failed tests, hidden labor, and social queues.
Fen’s expertise saves the system while remaining unentered; Sela salvages bent nails for her mother’s roof.
Tervan learns to rely on an unrecognized worker; Sela’s private plans compete with public necessity.
The runnel creates a durable settlement fact.
22
A Name for Work
Roan / Fen
Work companies create accountability, ration access, injury gaps, fraud, and representation.
Fen can rescue the work without being represented by it.
Roan sees that a system designed to include workers has created another gate.
No company system repairs Fen’s personhood.
23
A Place on the Plan
Yrsa / Sela
Planning makes firebreaks, graves, markets, doors, and removals politically visible.
Yrsa consumes six hours Sela intended for home and the laboring goat; apology cannot return the day.
Sela and Yrsa respect one another without erasing cost.
Planning is not permission, but the plan changes behavior.
24
Permanent Pegs
Yrsa / Tomas / Sela
Temporary plans become streets, ovens, political representation, and competing land claims.
Whitehart has materially enabled permanence while denying the word.
Sela refuses a plot and returns home; Tomas records material acts rather than a clean legal conclusion.
Ownership and settlement status remain unresolved.
25
The Elbow
Orla / Whitehart
Orla distinguishes what Whitehart may lawfully do from what it ought to do.
The ruling permits closure but does not cause or moralize it.
Merta asks whether Col knew; Orla refuses comfort she cannot prove.
Legal permission is not moral vindication.
26
What He Left Running
Perrin / Sela / Fen
Perrin leaves systems, requests, debts, questions, and a record in motion.
Sela refuses his invitation; Fen gives a handshake; the unexplained “four years” points toward Jubilee pressure.
Perrin departs without emotional closure or ownership of Fen.
Final authority of his record remains open.
27
Nine Days
Sela / senior table
Nine objects represent nine days of combined food; every defensible plan saves some and abandons others.
Sela learns the exact reach of her informal authority and deliberately does not stop Alaine’s order.
Alaine tells Sela the order is hers unless stopped; responsibility cannot be cleanly divided.
The gate closure is Whitehart’s act, not prophecy.
28
The Gate
Sela / collective
Five days of closure turn ration, negotiation, mercy, weather, and movement into a crush.
Accounts preserve incompatible first blows; the inward-opening gate becomes physically blocked.
Sela washes bodies with Fen; work replaces reconciliation.
Nine bodies and twelve missing remain separate; first aggressor unresolved.
29
The Keeper
Fen / Wilda
Wilda reveals she carried Col toward survival and frightened him east.
Fen learns the truth but cannot enter it through ordinary process.
Wilda transfers a burden without asking Fen to own or publish it.
Col’s survival remains unconfirmed.
30
I Don’t Want To
Fen / Merta
Fen tells Merta both what he sold and what Wilda said.
Merta receives a real choice, route knowledge, and Col’s coat without false hope.
Fen refuses to convert truth into payment or public record.
Merta will attempt the pass but turn back after Book One.
31
The Divided Record
Wren
Wren reconstructs gate sequence, conditions, maintenance, and source limits.
She separates identification data from future-use data and divides her report to reduce harm.
Her method changes from extraction toward controlled incompleteness.
Report becomes working material, not authoritative Chronicle text.
32
That’s How You Know
Sela / Nella / Tam
Grain arrives through prior credit; Nella publishes costs and profit; false words detach from Corrin.
Halla receives grain with an open refusal clause; Corrin is beaten and permanently loses long-verse performance.
Sela refuses Nella’s paid road seat; Tam’s violence changes Corrin and himself.
False quotation survives its original singer.
33
The Blank Page
Sela / Tomas / Ivet
Tomas refuses to enter a judgment; Tam returns Harl’s knife; Sela prepares to leave.
The blank page becomes an active refusal, not indecision.
Sela and Ivet achieve intimate farewell without restoring innocence.
Selas Ledger page remains blank.
34
East
Alaine / Sela
Alaine chooses not to call Sela back; Sela leaves by the ugly eastern way.
The Bell sounds beyond Whitehart’s ordinary count, but Sela cannot know what ended.
Sela exits Whitehart as a person with hunger, injury, knife, and uncertain road—not as succession solution.
Bell unexplained; Sela reaches Vey later under separate continuity.
4. End-of-Book knowledge state
Publicly usable: Whitehart closed the gate; bodies were received; Sela’s page is blank; grain arrived; a false quotation circulates; the Bell sounded.
Working but non-authoritative: Wren’s divided gate report, Perrin’s collected record, competing route and rescue accounts.
Privately held: Wilda’s account of Col is known to Wilda, Fen, and Merta; Sela knows why she refused Nella; Tomas knows the page is blank by deliberate choice.
Permanently unresolved: objective magic, first aggressor, Col’s final fate, objective Bell meaning, complete Eleventh truth.
