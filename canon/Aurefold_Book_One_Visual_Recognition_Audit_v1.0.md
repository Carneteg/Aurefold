## Test

A recurring character passes visual recognition if a reader could plausibly identify the person from one or two recurring embodied cues without the narration immediately supplying the name. Cues may be facial, bodily, clothing-based, work-based, injury-based, gestural, or object-linked, but must arise naturally from the life the character lives.

The audit does **not** require facial catalogues. A character can pass through a strong prop-and-behavior signature if that signature is already specific enough to function as recognition architecture.

## Blind spots found and repaired

### Brem — Chapter Four
**Problem:** High-frequency family character with strong dialogue and relationship identity but almost no body-level recognition before his later injury.

**New recognition anchors:** oversized adolescent hands; body still folding under the low table; one stubborn lock of hair at the crown.

**Reason:** He should read as Sela's twelve-year-old brother before the text says his name, without making him a miniaturized version of Sela.

### Sinnet — Chapter Eighteen
**Problem:** Important emotional witness to Fen's institutional erasure, but visually close to a generic kitchen worker.

**New recognition anchors:** flour-grey apron at one hip; split thumbnail holding a permanent white line.

**Reason:** The details come directly from repeated bread work and make her memorable without beautifying or sentimentalizing her.

### Hessa Morn — Chapter Twenty-Six
**Problem:** Strong material identity through cart/chest/grandchildren, but too little embodied recognition during her confrontation with planning authority.

**New recognition anchors:** hand laid flat on the iron-bound chest lid; chin pushed forward a fraction whenever the cart is discussed.

**Reason:** Her resistance becomes physically legible without inventing eye colour, hair colour, exact age, or symbolic nobility.

### Dova Sen — Chapter Thirty-Two
**Problem:** Functional healer risk; could blur with Serenel's bone woman or Maren.

**New recognition anchors:** sleeves rolled past elbow despite cold; clean linen looped twice around left wrist before it is needed.

**Reason:** Readiness becomes visible as habit. No miraculous-healer aura is introduced.

### Daven — Chapter Forty-Three
**Problem:** Important late Fen relationship/history but visually generic novice.

**New recognition anchors:** cuffs turned to equal width; centered belt knot; carefully arranged novice grey.

**Reason:** His body/clothes embody the successful accommodation to institutional rules that separates him from Fen, without turning him into a caricature.

## Deliberately not expanded

Characters with very small scene-only roles do not receive appearance beats merely to complete a database. Excess description would weaken scene focus. The guiding rule remains: **recognition where emotional, relational, or plot recurrence justifies memory.**

## Result

Five genuine visual blind spots repaired. No broad descriptive pass is authorized by this audit. Future appearance additions should be triggered by a specific recognition failure, not by a desire for uniform dossier completeness.
