# 1. Authority, Purpose and Interpretation

1.1  This file is binding subordinate canon for the implementation of Book One revisions and for the presentation of character, voice, House identity, and lived culture where those matters are not already fixed by higher authority.

1.2  It does not amend, replace, renumber, or create any numbered Canon Lock. Constitution v1.9 remains supreme. The Canon Ledger remains the audit/index instrument described by the Constitution.

1.3  Where this file conflicts with the Constitution, Canon Ledger, ratified series architecture, or an applicable higher governing file, the higher source wins. Silent reconciliation is forbidden.

1.4  The governing Book One manuscript remains Master v1.6 until a later manuscript version is formally adopted. This file directs revision of that manuscript; it does not itself rewrite entered story facts.

1.5  The purpose of the revision is not to replace the novel’s premise, metaphysical ambiguity, political architecture, or ending. The purpose is to increase human attachment, voice differentiation, rhythm, lived reality, and emotional consequence while preserving the novel’s successful structural and canonical foundations.

# 2. Governing Revision Principle

Directive BR-01 — Character before interpretation

Characters must not function primarily as vessels through which the novel explains institutions, mysteries, or philosophies. In every major scene, the human being must retain private appetites, irritations, loyalties, fears, bodily needs, relationships, and contradictions that exist beyond the scene’s thematic function.

Authority basis: Constitution v1.9, especially Canon Locks #021, #023, #028, #045 and Historical Narrative: Characters come before plot.

Status: BINDING

Directive BR-02 — No foundation rewrite

Book One revision is a targeted character-and-rhythm revision. The established premise, core mystery architecture, House structure, metaphysical uncertainty, and successful final movement are to be preserved unless a verified continuity error or higher-authority conflict requires correction.

Authority basis: Creator adoption in this file; Constitution v1.9 method: Emotion before Spectacle, Consequences before Convenience, Mystery before Explanation.

Status: BINDING

# 3. Book One Binding Revision Directives

BR-03 — Sela must possess private agency

Sela must repeatedly want, choose, avoid, protect, resent, need, or pursue things that are not reducible to the mystery surrounding her. Institutional reactions to Sela may remain central, but the narrative must not allow her to become merely the object of other people’s interpretation. Her practical, grounded, dry, and institutionally skeptical character should be strengthened through action, observation, humor, bodily reality, and small decisions with consequences.

Authority basis: Canon Lock #045; Book One Master v1.6; active Book One character-control files.

Status: BINDING

BR-04 — POV voices must be independently recognizable

Major POVs must have distinct cognitive and verbal fingerprints strong enough that substantial passages can often be identified without a POV label. Differentiation must arise from what each character notices, ignores, measures, fears, jokes about, values, and refuses—not merely from vocabulary substitutions.

Authority basis: Canon Locks #023 and #045; Book One character identity control.

Status: BINDING

BR-04A — Required Book One voice fingerprints

| Character | Binding fingerprint | Primary danger to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sela | Concrete, bodily, pragmatic, earthy; dry humor and irony, especially toward institutions and people performing institutional importance. | Becoming an abstract symbol or passive mystery-object. |
| Tomas | Extremely precise, testing-minded, corrective, pedantic, dry; frequently unintentionally funny because he applies exactness where life resists it. | Sounding generically wise or emotionally polished. |
| Wren | Method, evidentiary discipline, error sources, distinction between observation and conclusion; attention to what can and cannot be known. | Turning every uncertainty into the same philosophical cadence as other POVs. |
| Fen | Social omission, who is counted, who is not counted, names and people institutions pass around or fail to register; human presence at the edges of systems. | Existing only as a thematic device for absence. |
| Alaine | Hierarchy, judgment, intelligence, dignity, ritual competence; sharp irony, satirical condescension, and the capacity to be both insightful and overconfident. | Becoming a solemn exposition engine. |
| Perrin | Record, custody, consequence, names, labor and the politics of what survives into history; Blackcrest thought embodied in choices rather than lectures. | Explaining the Chronicle instead of living its moral costs. |

Status: BINDING

BR-05 — Compress exposition before adding plot

The principal pacing repair in the early and middle Book One movement is selective compression, not the automatic addition of new plot. Chapters and passages that primarily transfer institutional, historical, or philosophical information must be tested for interpersonal change. Where the same meaning has already been shown through action, setting, dialogue, or consequence, explanatory repetition should be cut or reduced.

Authority basis: Canon Lock #028; Constitution method: History before Plot does not require exposition before emotion.

Status: BINDING

BR-06 — The middle corridor must earn momentum

The stretch broadly corresponding to the manuscript’s early-middle and middle institutional corridor—especially chapters previously identified in review as information-dense—must be revised for forward emotional pressure. A scene may teach the reader about Aurefold, but it must also alter a relationship, decision, danger, loyalty, status, or self-understanding.

Authority basis: Creator-approved review conclusion; exact chapter surgery remains an editorial implementation decision, not a story canon fact.

Status: BINDING

BR-07 — Aftermath is story, not delay

Major violence, death, humiliation, institutional coercion, public accusation, and irreversible choice must create lived aftermath. The novel should show work continuing, bodies recovering or failing to recover, changed sleep, altered trust, awkward meals, guilt, anger, practical replacement of the dead, political appropriation, and the social consequences of what happened. Aftermath scenes must not exist solely to summarize emotion; they must change people and relationships.

Authority basis: Canon Locks #024 and #028; Constitution v1.9 Violence and Historical Reality principles.

Status: BINDING

BR-08 — Lower the constant narrative intensity

Aurefold must contain ordinary life in sufficient quantity to create contrast with crisis. Food, work, animals, weather, clothing, fatigue, attraction, embarrassment, friendship, jokes, petty resentment, boredom, maintenance, mistakes, and mundane competence are legitimate story material when they reveal character or culture. Not every paragraph, image, or exchange should carry maximal symbolic or philosophical weight.

Authority basis: Canon Locks #011, #012, #021, #022, #023 and #027; Historical Narrative: Personal before epic.

Status: BINDING

BR-09 — Vary chapter-ending rhetoric

The manuscript must not rely repeatedly on aphoristic, negating, paradoxical, or thesis-like closing sentences. Strong sententious endings remain permitted, but their frequency must be controlled. Chapters should also end on action, image, silence, interruption, humor, physical detail, unresolved gesture, mundane consequence, or emotional dislocation.

Authority basis: Creator-approved review conclusion; prose implementation directive.

Status: BINDING

BR-10 — Repair verified continuity errors only after proof

A continuity correction must be grounded in the governing manuscript and applicable canon before it is made. AI-review flags are not facts. Verified errors should be repaired with the smallest natural change that restores continuity. Unverified flags must not trigger rewrites.

Authority basis: Constitution v1.9 authority order and prohibition on silent reconciliation; current source-governance protocol.

Status: BINDING

BR-11 — Fen must be physically established before speaking in the Ledger-room sequence

The Book One manuscript contains a verified staging/continuity defect in which Fen speaks in the Ledger-room sequence without adequate prior establishment of his presence. The next manuscript revision must correct this by naturally establishing his presence before the line, without unnecessary exposition or alteration of the scene’s purpose.

Authority basis: Verified Book One review finding against Master v1.6.

Status: BINDING CORRECTION

BR-12 — Protect the final movement

The final movement is not to be expanded merely to explain mysteries, heighten spectacle, or make the ending more explicit. Ivets death, Tomas’s key-related material, Sela’s departure, and the final eastward movement should be preserved in function and restraint unless a higher-authority continuity correction is required. The ending must retain ambiguity where canon requires ambiguity.

Authority basis: Book One locked outcomes; Constitution Canon Locks #030, #041-#044; governing Master v1.6.

Status: BINDING

# 4. Binding World-Presentation Directives

WR-01 — Do not solve weak embodiment with more lore

When Aurefold feels thin on the page, the first remedy should be lived embodiment rather than additional macro-history. New lore should be created only when it serves story, civilization, continuity, or future architecture. The world must feel discovered through behavior, labor, custom, language, objects, institutions, disagreement, and consequence—not through accumulation for its own sake.

Authority basis: Canon Locks #011, #027, #057; Constitution method: World before Story, Civilization before Kingdom, History before Plot.

Status: BINDING

WR-02 — Every House must be perceptible before it is explained

A Great House should become recognizable through ordinary behavior and social texture before or beyond explicit philosophical explanation. House identity must appear in how people allocate work, raise children, insult rivals, mourn, bargain, punish, forgive, organize risk, remember debt, display status, treat strangers, and respond to failure.

Authority basis: Canon Locks #038, #039, #045, #057.

Status: BINDING

WR-03 — Every Great House requires three verbal layers

Each Great House must develop three distinguishable verbal registers: (1) official House language used by institutions and public authority; (2) common language—sayings, practical phrases, jokes, warnings, proverbs, insults, and shorthand used by ordinary people; and (3) external or hostile language used by rivals, victims, skeptics, and outsiders. These layers must grow from House philosophy but may contradict one another. Individual phrases do not become canon until separately approved and entered into the relevant House or civilization file.

Authority basis: Canon Locks #038, #039, #045 and #057.

Status: BINDING FRAMEWORK; PHRASES REQUIRE SEPARATE ADOPTION

WR-04 — Internal dissent is mandatory within every House

A House may share a governing philosophy without sharing a single interpretation of what that philosophy requires. Meaningful internal factions, class differences, regional readings, generational conflicts, institutional rivalries, and private dissent must exist. House-versus-House conflict must never replace conflict within a House.

Authority basis: Canon Locks #039, #045, #050, #054, #056 and #057.

Status: BINDING

WR-05 — Philosophy must become material culture

House philosophy must produce practical consequences in tools, records, contracts, roads, workshops, ports, kitchens, burial practice, military organization, architecture, labor systems, education, household rules, succession practice, and other material institutions. Worldbuilding should repeatedly translate abstract leadership philosophy into something a person must touch, obey, maintain, benefit from, pay for, or suffer under.

Authority basis: Canon Locks #032, #039, #049, #057 and the Technology & Material Life governing baseline.

Status: BINDING

WR-06 — Whitehart must create undeniable human good

Because Book One exposes the dangers of Faith as classification, interpretation, ritual authority, and institutional control, Whitehart must also be shown producing genuine and material good sufficient to make intelligent, decent allegiance credible. Its civilization must visibly care for, feed, bury, comfort, organize, forgive, teach, preserve, or protect people in ways that matter. This good must not erase its victims, and its victims must not erase its good.

Authority basis: Canon Locks #033 and #039; Book One Whitehart dominance.

Status: BINDING

WR-07 — No Great House may become a thesis with costumes

Characters are not philosophical mouthpieces. A person from a House may betray, misunderstand, parody, exploit, reinterpret, resent, or barely think about the House philosophy. Class, appetite, family, love, fear, ambition, injury, profession, region, and circumstance may matter more to an individual than formal ideology.

Authority basis: Canon Lock #045.

Status: BINDING

WR-08 — Living Archive material remains non-canon unless separately ratified

Living Archive material may be mined for situations, tensions, historical texture, or candidate ideas, but the package does not become canon through reuse or citation. Any adopted element must pass the current authority process. The stale Living Archive Canon Adoption file has no power to ratify material against the v1.9 authority line.

Authority basis: Current v1.9 source governance and active consolidation/source-registry status.

Status: BINDING SOURCE-GOVERNANCE RULE

# 5. Protected Elements — Do Not Change Through This Revision

The world/series name Aurefold and the administrative/ancient-imperial use of Aurelion.

The ten recognized Great Houses and their governing philosophies.

The decentralized Tenfold Compact and the Hall of Eleven structure.

The objective uncertainty surrounding magic and all apparent supernatural events.

The permanent nature of death and the absence of literal resurrection.

The unresolved nature of the erased Eleventh House and all Canon Lock protections surrounding it.

The separation between nine bodies and twelve missing in Book One.

The absence of an objectively established first attacker at the gate.

The inward-opening gate.

Cols survival remaining unconfirmed.

Selas Ledger page remaining blank.

Sela leaving east with Harls knife.

The bell strokes remaining objectively unexplained in Book One.

The principle that no recognized Great House is singularly evil and each must create both genuine good and genuine victims.

These protections are inherited from higher or existing canon. Their appearance here does not create duplicate canon; it establishes the revision boundary.

# 6. Revision Priority Model

| Priority | Required work | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P0 | Verified continuity and staging repairs | Remove factual defects without collateral rewriting. |
| P1 | POV differentiation; Sela private agency; selective middle compression; stronger aftermath | Increase attachment, momentum, and human consequence. |
| P2 | Lower constant intensity; more ordinary life and humor; varied chapter endings | Create contrast, realism, memorability, and tonal range. |
| P3 | House verbal layers; internal dissent; material culture; lived microculture | Make Aurefold inhabitable and fandom-capable without lore inflation. |
| PROTECT | Core ending, ambiguity architecture, House foundations, metaphysical limits | Prevent revision from damaging what already works. |

# 7. Revision Test

Can a reader identify the POV from cognition and attention, not only from names?

Does Sela make private choices that would matter to her even if the mystery vanished?

Does each information-heavy scene also alter a person, relationship, decision, danger, or status?

Do major harms produce consequences beyond the scene in which they occur?

Does the manuscript contain enough ordinary life to make crisis feel different from normal life?

Are chapter endings rhythmically varied?

Can a House be felt in ordinary behavior before its philosophy is explained?

Can an intelligent, decent person defend each recognized House from within the story?

Can the reader also see who pays for that House’s virtues?

Has any proposed lore been accidentally promoted to canon without the required authority path?
