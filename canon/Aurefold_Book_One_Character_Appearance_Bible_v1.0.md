**Canon effect:** No numbered Canon Lock is created. This file consolidates established visual facts and adds author-facing descriptive presentation guidance. Where a painterly paragraph goes beyond literal physical facts, it is a prose target, not a new biographical datum.

## Purpose

Aurefold characters should be visually memorable as people, not as fantasy archetypes or costume dossiers. Physical description should emerge through viewpoint, work, injury, weather, status, clothing use, posture, and what another character would actually notice.

Use these descriptions as **recognition architecture**, not as paragraphs to paste wholesale into the manuscript.

### Rules

1. Preserve established visual facts.
2. Do not add eye colour, exact height, scars, tattoos, jewelry, disabilities, ancestry markers, or other hard physical facts unless separately approved or already supported by the governing manuscript.
3. Re-description should vary by POV. Sela notices work, weather, hands, weight, and cost. Wren notices measurable distinguishing features. Perrin notices social effect. Alaine notices status and self-presentation.
4. Prefer one or two vivid details on first encounter, then recurring recognition details later.
5. Beauty should not become generic symmetry. Faces should carry work, age, weather, injury, class, and habit.
6. Clothing should look used by the life the character actually lives.
7. Avoid modern beauty-language and cinematic slow-motion description.
8. Description must never imply supernatural essence, destiny, or moral truth.

## Sela of the Light Heights

**Established visual signature:** Lean, compact working build; wind-browned nose; grey-green eyes; thin white notch through the left eyebrow; practical brown hair usually braided; red, callused hands.

**Painterly prose target:**  
Sela looked made for slopes rather than rooms. Nothing about her was delicate, though at a distance the narrowness of her frame could suggest it; up close there was the compact strength of someone who carried feed uphill, wrestled stubborn animals, and expected her body to be useful before it was admired. Wind had browned the bridge of her nose more deeply than the rest of her face. Her eyes were grey-green and watchful without trying to look watchful. A thin white notch cut through her left eyebrow, small enough to miss until she frowned. Her brown hair was normally braided for work, not beauty, and her hands were the least ambiguous thing about her: red from weather, thickened at the useful places, and incapable of pretending she had spent her life indoors.

**Recognition motifs:** Hands; braid; white eyebrow notch; wind-browned skin; compact physical competence.

**On-page use:** Describe her through what work has done to her body. Avoid lingering admiration from neutral narration. Other characters may beautify, sanctify, diminish, or misread her; the prose itself should remain materially grounded.

## Tomas

**Established visual signature:** Forty-five; narrow long face and hands; dark hair greying and receding unevenly; close beard trimmed with irritating precision; charcoal-marked fingers.

**Painterly prose target:**  
At forty-five, Tomas had gone narrow rather than heavy. His face was long, his hands longer, and both seemed designed for pointing out distinctions other people hoped would pass unnoticed. His dark hair had begun to retreat unevenly at the temples and grey without consulting the rest of him. The beard was kept close with a precision bordering on accusation. His fingers were rarely clean; charcoal lived in the creases and beside the nails no matter how often he wiped them. At first glance he could seem severe. The longer one watched him, the more the severity resolved into concentration — a man forever trying to keep the world from smearing one category into another.

**Recognition motifs:** Long hands; exact beard; uneven greying; charcoal marks; slightly over-controlled grooming.

**On-page use:** Let physical precision echo procedural precision, but never make him visually comic. The humor comes from his seriousness.

## Alaine Serenel

**Established visual signature:** Iron-white hair braided flat; large expressive eyes; pared older face; walking stick; physical stillness that can be mistaken for holiness.

**Painterly prose target:**  
Age had pared Alaine rather than softened her. Her face had lost whatever fullness it once possessed, leaving the bones, the mouth, and the enormous Serenel eyes to do almost all the speaking. Her hair was iron-white and braided flat with the discipline of a habit older than many of the people who obeyed her. She carried a walking stick without inviting help and could hold herself so still that strangers mistook the absence of movement for serenity. People who knew her better watched the eyes. They were too alive for sainthood: judging, amused, wounded, impatient, and sometimes all four before her mouth had chosen one.

**Recognition motifs:** Iron-white flat braid; expressive Serenel eyes; walking stick; unnerving stillness.

**On-page use:** Her body should create tension between age, authority, fragility, and control. Never describe her as literally holy or supernaturally imposing; that is an interpretation characters may make.

## Corven Serenel

**Established visual signature:** Large expressive Serenel eyes; warm-brown hair that catches gold in firelight; road-cut hair; physical competence beneath expensive status; often visibly injured or working.

**Painterly prose target:**  
Corven carried the Serenel eyes without Alaine's stillness. On him they were expansive rather than withholding, quick to find whoever had spoken and quicker to make that person feel worth the finding. His hair was warm brown, cut for roads rather than portraits, and fire brought gold out of it that daylight did not promise. Nothing he wore was cheap, but expensive cloth on Corven rarely remained ceremonial for long: sleeves were pushed back, hems dirtied, good leather bent by use. He had the disconcerting habit of looking more convincing while carrying timber or bleeding through a bandage than many highborn men did from a dais.

**Recognition motifs:** Serenel eyes; warm-brown road-cut hair; expensive materials used hard; visible work or injury.

**On-page use:** His attractiveness should come primarily from vitality, attention, and competence rather than a catalogue of handsome features. His charisma must feel embodied and real.

## Perrin

**Established visual signature:** Dark thinning hair; brown eyes that warm before his mouth; once-broken nose; travel coat; socially attentive face.

**Painterly prose target:**  
Perrin was neither handsome enough to make charm effortless nor plain enough to disappear. His dark hair had begun thinning at the crown, and his nose had been broken once and allowed to remain slightly wrong. The brown eyes mattered more. They warmed a fraction before he smiled, giving the impression that pleasure in another person had occurred to him before courtesy required it. A travel coat seemed more natural on him than formal dress, as though he was always between one conversation and the next. His face was built less for beauty than for permission: people looked at it and found themselves explaining one detail more than they had intended.

**Recognition motifs:** Warm-before-smile eyes; broken nose; thinning hair; travel coat; attentive orientation toward speakers.

**On-page use:** Description should support the ambiguity between genuine interest and professional extraction. Do not make him slick, predatory, or omniscient.

## Wren

**Established visual signature:** Tall and spare; black hair cut at the jaw; old pale burn across two right knuckles; economical movement.

**Painterly prose target:**  
Wren was tall and spare enough that stillness made her seem even taller. Her black hair ended at the jaw because anything longer would have become another thing to move out of the way. An old pale burn crossed two knuckles of her right hand, noticeable mostly because her hands otherwise did exactly what she intended and very little else. She wasted no motion reaching for paper, turning an object, or stepping around someone. The economy could read as coldness until one understood that Wren treated her own body the way she treated evidence: unnecessary movement was another source of contamination.

**Recognition motifs:** Jaw-cut black hair; pale burn across right knuckles; spare frame; controlled economical motion.

**On-page use:** Avoid giving her generic “sharp” eyes unless a POV specifically reads her that way. Her distinctiveness is method embodied.

## Fen Leren

**Established visual signature:** Narrow chest; permanent work stoop; near-black hair falling over one eye; wax-scaled hands; mouth that looks amused before he is.

**Painterly prose target:**  
Fen had been shaped by work that buildings were designed to notice more readily than workers. He was narrow through the chest and carried a permanent stoop earned from doorways built for loads rather than men. Near-black hair kept falling over one eye, and he pushed it back only when it actually interfered. Wax lived on his hands in pale scales, lodged across the knuckles and in the lines of the skin. His mouth often seemed on the verge of amusement before the rest of his face agreed, which made strangers mistake bitterness for ease. People who worked beside him learned the difference.

**Recognition motifs:** Work stoop; hair over one eye; wax-scaled hands; almost-amused mouth.

**On-page use:** His physicality should show invisible labor without turning him into a symbol of suffering. He remains capable, funny, manipulative, tired, cruel, generous, and ordinary.

## Orla Darun

**Established visual signature:** Small relative to Darun portraits; broad jaw; deep-set dark eyes; decisive nose; short iron-grey hair; road-red skin; repeatedly mended coat.

**Painterly prose target:**  
Orla Darun looked smaller than the portraits had prepared people for and more difficult to move. She had a broad jaw, a nose that seemed to arrive at decisions before the rest of her face, and deep-set dark eyes beneath short iron-grey hair. The road lived plainly on her: skin reddened by wind, a coat mended too many times to pretend the repairs were temporary, dust caught where expensive travelers usually paid someone else to remove it. Nothing about her asked to be impressive. That was part of the impression.

**Recognition motifs:** Compact scale; broad jaw; decisive nose; iron-grey hair; road-red skin; mended coat.

**On-page use:** Resist grandeur. Her authority should sharpen when she appears least ceremonial.

## Tam

**Established visual signature:** Seventeen; long wrists; unfinished shoulders; Harl's heavy brows; hair that will not stay tied.

**Painterly prose target:**  
At seventeen, Tam looked as though his body had not finished negotiating with itself. His wrists had lengthened before his shoulders knew what to do with them, and strength appeared in awkward places first. Harl's heavy brows sat on a face too young to have earned their severity. His hair refused every knot he put it in and worked loose again before the morning was properly underway. He could look almost boyish until he lifted something heavy without thinking, or anger arrived faster than language and made the resemblance to Harl less comforting.

**Recognition motifs:** Long wrists; unfinished shoulders; Harl's brows; perpetually escaping hair; sudden physical competence.

**On-page use:** Keep youth visible even after violence. Do not visually age him into a hardened warrior because the plot becomes harsher.

## Aren Lethren

**Established visual signature:** Broad rather than tall; close-shaved; one flattened ear; aristocratic Lethren nose; excellent clothing visibly used hard.

**Painterly prose target:**  
Aren Lethren was broad rather than tall, built like a man accustomed to occupying the useful part of a doorway. His head was close-shaved, exposing one ear flattened by an old break and making the fine Lethren nose look almost misplaced on the rest of him. His clothing was excellent and treated with no reverence at all: good wool creased by travel, better boots carrying mud, fastenings repaired before they became decorative failures. He looked highborn only until he began counting stores. Then he looked like what rank had made possible rather than what rank was for.

**Recognition motifs:** Broad frame; flattened ear; aristocratic nose; close shave; high-quality clothes used as tools.

**On-page use:** Let class and administrative competence coexist visually. Avoid making him look militaristic by default; his danger lies in systems as much as weapons.

## Ivet

**Established visual signature:** Young novice; expressive face; hair escaping its knot; three uneven blue repair stitches on her cuff as a key recognition mark.

**Painterly prose target:**  
Ivet always looked a little less arranged than Whitehart preferred. Her hair escaped its knot in soft rebellions through the day, and her face showed reactions before training could decide whether they were suitable. The blue repair stitches on one cuff were uneven enough to notice and sturdy enough to hold, three small marks made by someone who cared more that the cloth survived than that the repair disappeared. She laughed too visibly, worried too visibly, and worked with the earnest concentration of a young person who had not yet learned to make usefulness look effortless.

**Recognition motifs:** Escaping hair; expressive face; three uneven blue cuff stitches; visible earnestness.

**On-page use:** Protect her ordinariness. Do not retroactively describe her with martyr-light, purity imagery, or visual foreshadowing of death.

## Manuscript Integration Rule

This Bible does **not** require every character to receive a full descriptive paragraph. During future prose revision or copy-level enrichment, use:
- 1–2 vivid details at first meaningful appearance;
- 1 recurring recognition detail in later scenes;
- POV-specific reinterpretation rather than repeated identical wording;
- new physical facts only after continuity review.
