## Method

The synthesis combines three evidence types and keeps them separate:

1. **Production scorecard facts:** the thirteen structured dimensions and six automatic signals from `author_scene_scorecards`.
2. **Deterministic manuscript measures:** story-unit word counts and final-paragraph form parsed directly from English Master v1.6.
3. **Editorial inference:** revision priorities derived from the first two layers and checked against protected epistemic boundaries.

The manuscript parser found 48 headed story units and approximately 105,302 words including chapter/interlude headings. This is an internal comparison measure, not a publishing word count.

## Whole-book result

Book One is not structurally inert. Every reviewed unit has clear or urgent desire and a meaningful or irreversible choice. Nearly every unit changes information, emotion, relationships or material conditions. The problem visible at whole-book scale is the opposite: the matrix says that almost everything is urgent, severe, major, irreversible, strong and structural.

That produces two simultaneous conclusions:

- The manuscript has unusually high causal interlock. Information changes behavior; institutional wording changes material life; private choices acquire public consequences.
- The present scorecard calibration has limited discriminatory power. It is good at proving that the book is active, but less good at showing where intensity rises, rests or changes kind.

### Ceiling-compression check

For a diagnostic comparison, eleven dimensions were marked as “top band” when they held urgent, severe, irreversible, major or structural. Hook and exposition were excluded because `cliff` and `dominant` are not simple quality maxima.

- 42 / 48 units occupy the top band on at least 8 of those 11 dimensions.
- 27 / 48 occupy the top band on at least 10 of 11.
- 17 / 48 occupy the top band on all 11.

This does not prove that the prose is melodramatic or that the judgments are wrong. It does show that the current scale cannot reliably distinguish the book's peaks from its baseline. Before v1.7 is assessed, the team should define representative anchors for `meaningful` versus `major`, `meaningful` versus `severe`, and `material` versus `structural` removal impact.

### 1. The long exposition corridor is Ch 3 through Ch 19

The scorecards contain one uninterrupted run of 18 high-exposition story units, beginning with Chapter 3's first unit and ending with Chapter 19. Twenty-six of the 27 high-exposition units also carry a strong or cliff exit. Twenty-three combine high exposition with at least eight top-band change values.

This is not an information-only corridor: all six conservative exposition-dominance signals remain false because the chapters also move choice, relationship, emotion, material state and reversal. The editorial risk is cumulative reader load and repeated explanatory cadence, not static lore delivery.

First-pass prose targets inside the corridor should be Chapters 9, 11, 13 and 16-19. These units repeatedly handle records, accounts, evidence, custody and public compression. The revision question is not “Can this information be removed?” but “Which explanation has already been embodied by an action, object, conflict or prior chapter and therefore no longer needs a second abstract statement?”

### 2. Chapters 19-24 form an intentional but extreme accordion

Chapter 21, *The Price of Water*, is the longest unit at approximately 5,070 words. It is followed by Chapters 22-24 at approximately 1,369, 1,413 and 1,575 words. The band therefore averages 2,542 words while containing the widest internal span in the book.

The long chapter earns its length through consent, diagnosis, water engineering, failure testing, debt, maintenance and Fen's hidden labor. A revision should preserve that causal chain. The practical line-edit target is duplicated procedural explanation, not the infrastructure itself. Conversely, the short Chapters 22-24 should not be padded to equalize length; they should be checked for whether the emotional and bodily aftermath has enough room before each next institutional turn.

### 3. The middle compresses; the ending expands again

Average unit length falls to 1,657 words in Chapters 25-30 and 1,483 in Chapters 31-36, then rises to 3,009 in Chapters 43-47. The shift supports acceleration through conflict and a more expansive reckoning at the end. It also creates a late-book risk: Chapters 43 and 45 carry dense account/quotation work just as the narrative is rebuilding length.

The correct test is experiential, not arithmetic: preserve the acceleration, then ensure the expanded final movement gives each return of record, report and disputed wording a distinct dramatic vehicle.

## Exit-cadence audit

The scorecard hook scale says 46 / 48 exits are strong or cliff and two are soft. A prose-level check reveals a separate pattern:

- 30 / 48 story units end with a final paragraph of 12 words or fewer;
- 19 / 48 final paragraphs contain an explicit negative term such as *no*, *not*, *nothing*, *nobody*, *never*, *neither* or *without*;
- Chapters 27-35 contain nine consecutive short final paragraphs;
- Chapters 20-24 and 39-43 each contain five consecutive short final paragraphs;
- Chapters 45-47 contain three consecutive short final paragraphs.

Many individual exits are strong. At sequence scale, however, repeated compressed verdicts and negative turns can make authored design visible. The revision should vary selected exits toward unfinished physical action, sensory residue, interrupted dialogue or unresolved spatial movement. The goal is not to weaken hooks; it is to prevent the same rhetorical machinery from announcing them.

Highest-value exit review: Chapters 27-35, then Chapters 39-47. Chapters 46-47 should be protected unless an alternative preserves the blank-page action, the opening wicket, Sela's eastward departure and the Bell's unexplained sounding.

## Agency and POV limit

At story-unit level, the book shows active focal agency: 48 / 48 scorecards have clear or urgent desire, and 48 / 48 have meaningful or irreversible choice. The qualitative records repeatedly distinguish action from interpretation—for example, Sela may command effectively without prophecy, and Wren may preserve incompatible accounts without owning total truth.

The database cannot yet support a true per-POV comparison. All 48 current Book One `pov_character_id` / `pov_name` values are null. Consequently, the `passive_pov_signal = 0` result is structurally non-probative: the predicate only applies when a POV character is populated.

Before any claim such as “Sela has less agency than Wren in the middle movement” is promoted from reading note to measured finding, Book One's story units need explicit focal/POV metadata. Until then, character-specific agency remains a text-checked editorial inference, not a database aggregate.

## Removal-cost interpretation

All 48 units are scored `structural` if removed. That may reflect the manuscript's genuine interlock, but a dimension with one value cannot rank revision candidates. The next calibration should distinguish:

- **structural:** removal breaks the causal, canonical or ending architecture and requires cross-book reconstruction;
- **material:** removal requires substantial local redistribution but not architectural redesign;
- **local:** the unit's function can be absorbed by adjacent material without changing the book's governing sequence.

This is a calibration recommendation, not an instruction to delete scenes.

## Protected-ambiguity guard for revision

Any prose revision generated from this synthesis must preserve all of the following:

- Sela can report, choose and act without objective proof of a supernatural voice or cause.
- Naming Col, receiving an account about Col or tracing a possible route does not prove that Col survived or died.
- The east-shoulder grave retains damaged provenance; no total count, identity or Eleventh solution may be reconstructed from it.
- Wren's identified Lower Field bolt does not settle moral or causal first aggression.
- Nine received bodies and twelve missing remain separate Gate reckonings, not one reconciled count.
- No single Gate aggressor or exclusive mechanical account of Ivet's death becomes objective knowledge.
- The false Sela quotation can become socially effective without becoming true.
- The Bell's sounding is text-entered; its cause, agency, meaning and divine status remain unresolved.
- Observation, inference, legal effect and public wording remain separate layers.

## Recommended revision queue

### Priority 1 — cumulative density

1. Read Chapters 3-19 as one corridor, not as isolated successes.
2. Begin line-level compression with Chapters 9, 11, 13 and 16-19.
3. Remove only explanatory duplication already carried by scene action, material evidence or a prior established rule.
4. Preserve every evidence limit and every distinction between true sentence, usable record and complete account.

### Priority 1 — rhythm at the existing issue range

1. Review Chapters 19-24 as a six-unit rhythm.
2. In Chapter 21, test a modest compression pass against the full water/consent/debt/maintenance causal chain.
3. In Chapters 22-24, test for bodily and relational dwell rather than adding explanation.
4. Preserve Sela's admission that command feels good; do not convert it into supernatural validation or simple corruption.

### Priority 2 — exit variation

1. Audit Chapters 27-35 and 39-47 for repeated short verdict endings.
2. Change only enough exits to break the visible pattern.
3. Prefer concrete continuation over an additional summarizing aphorism where the scene has already made its point.

### Priority 2 — character differentiation

1. Populate Book One focal/POV metadata before running character-level agency analytics.
2. During prose revision, test whether Sela, Wren, Fen, Tomas and Alaine reach uncertainty through character-specific perception and syntax rather than a shared institutional-summary voice.
3. Preserve methodological worldbuilding, but move repeated explanation into the character whose labor, body or relationship pays for it.

### Priority 3 — scorecard calibration

Use a representative anchor set before assessing Master v1.7: Chapters 4, 11, 19, 21, 27, 35, 42 and 47. Agree on what the middle bands mean, then recalibrate only if the evidence supports a change. A lower score is not automatically an improvement, and a high score is not approval.

## Decision

Book One is ready to move from coverage to selective revision. The recommended next implementation is a controlled manuscript revision pass, starting with cumulative exposition in Chapters 3-19 and rhythm in Chapters 19-24, followed by an exit-variation pass. No canon file should change merely because this synthesis exists. No Supabase schema or scorecard row needs to change until POV metadata or an explicit recalibration is approved.
