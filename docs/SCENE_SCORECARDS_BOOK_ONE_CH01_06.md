## Scene-level conclusions

### Chapter 1 - The Trial of the Goatherd

Tomas tries to conduct an exact trial without letting Sela's answers prove themselves. Sela refuses the expected performance, Tomas withholds judgment and burns the temporary note, and Alaine provides a discipline for uncertainty rather than an explanation. The scene ends when Tomas recognizes that the inquiry tested Sela's uncertainty but not Alaine's certainty.

Protected remainder: Sela reports an experience. The shared turn toward the arch, her report and the trial procedure do not verify a voice, supernatural cause or objective magical knowledge.

### Chapter 2 - The Woman Against the Stone

The reader sees the practiced construction behind Alaine's public stillness. Her care for Sela and the house cannot be separated from institutional self-protection, and the compassionate phrase `You already have` becomes dangerous because an office can turn reassurance into doctrine.

Protected remainder: Alaine's experiences and interpretations remain unverifiable. Private interior knowledge about her uncertainty is not proof of magic.

### Chapter 3 - The Morning After

The trial reorganizes Sela's ordinary life before any doctrine is formally declared. Work is taken from her, visitors commodify proximity, and ordinary objects acquire meaning. Sela repairs the break with Ivet through goat care and takes up the broom, recovering action without recovering the old social meaning.

Protected remainder: reverence and reclassification are social acts performed by other people; they do not confirm the truth of Sela's claimed experience.

### Chapter 4 - Harl's Road

Harl's family gives Sela ordinary treatment through road work, horse care and accountable deposits. At the same time, a pilgrimage economy begins organizing itself around her reputation. The quiet exit permits Sela a small, practical desire to see Tam earn his horse.

Protected remainder: the road, knife and family material are practical and relational. They are not prophetic signs.

### Chapter 5 - The Ledger

Sela learns that the Ledger preserves judgment and legal effect while the observations behind them are burned. Fen's unfinished page spared death but made absence of classification into a durable social status he never consented to bear. Tomas can name the harm, but ordinary process cannot reopen the page.

Protected remainder: Sela's own judgment remains absent. Fen's survival and labor do not convert the blank page into a positive classification.

### Chapter 6 - Col

Sela restores Col's name as Merta's son without claiming knowledge of his fate. Tomas cannot reconstruct the burned evidence behind his old red judgment and privately repeats the forbidden name. The act begins moving through Whitehart as information and social consequence, not proof.

Protected remainder: Col's survival or death remains unresolved. Naming him is not supernatural evidence, and Tomas's later uncertainty does not recreate the destroyed trial record.
