# 3. Core creative philosophy

Series principles repeatedly reinforced by the owner and Constitution:
- World before Story
- Civilization before Kingdom
- History before Plot
- Emotion before Spectacle
- Consequences before Convenience
- Mystery before Explanation
- Readers before Trends

Core series question:
> **How should humanity live together?**

The story must never tell readers which Great House has the morally correct answer.

Tone target:
- mature, serious and materially grounded;
- not sanitized;
- not shock-for-shock grimdark;
- violence may be ugly, explicit and historically grounded when it reveals character, institutional truth, political reality or consequence;
- aftermath matters as much as the violent event;
- no plot-convenient recovery from major harm.

The owner explicitly approved historically grounded depictions of punishment, coercion, captivity, torture, sexual violence, war crimes and comparable harms when narratively earned. Detail and discomfort are legitimate tools; harm must not be eroticized or celebrated.

# 5. Political / metaphysical / technological hard boundaries

## Tenfold Compact
- decentralized;
- no emperor;
- no permanent throne;
- no imperial army;
- Hall of Eleven has eleven places but ten recognized House voices/votes;
- the eleventh place remains unexplained.

## Eleventh House / Ashen Crown
The erased Eleventh House and Ashen Crown mystery must never be solved objectively.

No single:
- relic,
- text,
- grave,
- archive,
- bloodline,
- witness,
- map,
- braid,
- prophecy,
- archaeological find
may provide the definitive answer.

Human, political, archival, mythic and potentially uncanny interpretations must remain viable. False leads are desirable; an earlier owner-approved authorial-black-file concept held that at least roughly one-third of apparent traces should be false or misleading.

Ashen Crown is not simply “the lost House revealed”.

## Magic/metaphysics
- Magic never objectively confirmed.
- Every uncanny occurrence retains a credible non-magical explanation.
- No hidden magic system later revealed as fact.
- Death permanent; no literal resurrection.
- Gods, souls and afterlife neither proven nor disproven.
- No verified prophet/chosen savior.
- Confirmed intelligent civilizations are human.

Sela must never become a verified prophetic key or an objectively correct supernatural interpreter.

## Technology
Approximate blend:
- 40% Renaissance
- 30% late medieval
- 20% antiquity
- 10% unique Aurefold innovation

Black powder exists unevenly and has not replaced bows, cavalry or close combat. No industrialization. Medicine is naturalistic. Houses differ through institutions/specialization, not by living in different technological eras.

# 6. History and source philosophy

History is not objective narration in Aurefold. Events are understood through:
- competing witnesses;
- archives;
- songs;
- trial records;
- Chronicle versions;
- maps;
- omissions;
- later political use.

Maps should carry date, maker, patron/purpose and omissions/limits.

Blackcrest may maintain the official Chronicle but does not own truth.

The owner wants historical events to mutate through retelling rather than receive a single authorial “correct answer” in public-facing lore.

The Living Archive Cycle is a strong proposal bank but remains proposal unless separately ratified. Its best function is to dramatize House virtues as material institutions rather than exposition. Do not silently import Living Archive names/events into canon.

# 13. Book One reader validation and market/literary balance

Two separate simulated-reader passes were requested after editorial closure and before Book Two work.

## Commercial epic-fantasy reader
Overall 8.7/10. Strong character attachment, especially Sela/Tomas/Fen/Alaine. Loved Lower Field causality and final Gate sequence. Main DNF/pacing risk: long institutional middle stretch (records, roads, water, provenance systems).

## Literary fantasy reader
Overall 9.1/10. Saw institutional material as the book's originality rather than a flaw. Praised Whitehart as lived belief/law/routine, Fen's invisibility, Merta/Col, the sense the world could continue without the protagonist, and the unresolved Bell.

Primary critique: many characters sometimes formulate dilemmas with suspiciously uniform intellectual precision; strong sentences can appear aware that they are strong.

## Combined conclusion
8.9/10. Shared underlying issue:
> **The book can sometimes be too aware of its own intelligence.**

Decision: do not automatically launch a generic revision/streamlining pass. What the commercial reader would cut is often what the literary reader considers the novel's identity. Protect distinctiveness unless a targeted reader-risk case is demonstrated.

# 18. Naming/register/lore hygiene

A previous major editorial concern was proper-name collision and near-duplicate control across Book One, Book Two and Living Archive material. Treat name reuse as a continuity risk, particularly when proposal lore creates near-homophones of protected main-cast names.

Living Archive proposal work previously triggered concerns around collisions such as Joren/Joric, Nera variants, Marr, Sela/Sella/Lessa, Fen/Fenn, Orren/Orrin, Varra/Vara, Tomas, etc. Do not assume those exact old collisions remain active after later repairs; the principle is to check the protected name register before introducing new proper nouns.
