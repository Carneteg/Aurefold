# 1. ARC LAW

1. **Change is visible before it is explained.** Behavior, work, silence, and altered habits carry the arc.
2. **Growth is not improvement.** A person may become harder, more compromised, or more capable of self-deception.
3. **Experience transfers.** A later chapter cannot behave as if an earlier wound did not occur.
4. **The small want carries the reader.** Philosophy taxes a human want the reader can recognize.
5. **The reader knows unevenly.** Knowledge asymmetry is a bond mechanism, not a puzzle to be solved quickly.
6. **A changed tell is canon.** Permanent changes to physical behavior require a Lore Bible update.
7. **No character summarizes their own completed arc.** The reader performs that work.

# 2. MOVEMENT STRUCTURE

- **Movement I — The Trial and After (Prologue–Chapter 8):** belief enters the house and rearranges ordinary life.
- **Movement II — The Strangers and the Record (Chapters 9–17):** Blackcrest, the valley economy, Fen's interior, Harl's death, and the struggle over who may be written.
- **Movement III — The Institution Breaks (Chapters 18–27):** law, hunger, the gate, confession, false history, the blank page, and east.

Alaine's three interludes remain Chapters 3, 17, and 26.

# 3. PRIMARY ARCS

## SELA — unwanted authority

### Small want
To be treated as an ordinary person who can speak plainly and still belong.

### Baseline
- Nineteen, illiterate, practical, trained by animals, weather, and slope.
- Faces openings and keeps the weather where she can see it.
- Believes truth is safer than performance.

### Movement I
- The trial makes her an object of interpretation.
- Breakfast proves that reverence can exile without cruelty.
- The Ledger shows that Whitehart can preserve life by removing personhood.
- She says Col's name because Merta asks for a human act, not a revelation.
- Her public denial of revelation becomes stronger proof than a claim would have been.

### Movement II
- Perrin's recording teaches her that a true detail can become someone else's tool.
- Her warning about wet ground becomes prohibition.
- She refuses the lie that would save Harl's pride and perhaps his life.
- Harl takes the neck and dies anyway. The wet spring remains sufficient.
- Her authority becomes inseparable from guilt, though the book never certifies that she caused the death.

### Movement III
- The valley and house use her words while disputing her meaning.
- The false quotation proves that even silence can be authored by others.
- She receives no verdict and therefore no permitted identity.
- She leaves without learning Fen sold her fear.

### Visible bends
- Public self and private self separate.
- She becomes more careful with speech, not more eloquent.
- She stops expecting honesty to control interpretation.
- At the end she acts without permission, verdict, or verified voice.

### End-state
More powerful in other people's stories, less certain in her own. She walks east because staying has become another form of consent.

### Carry-forward scars
- Harl's knife.
- Tam's horse that never came.
- The false quotation.
- Fen's missing confession.
- The knowledge that institutions can turn mercy into disappearance.

## TOMAS — the instrument that sees its crack

### Small want
One verdict he can remember without returning to the gap beneath it.

### Baseline
- Forty-five.
- Sixteen years a Tester, nine years the Tester.
- Trusts disciplined observation and the separation of evidence from verdict.
- Burns working notes because he believes the rule protects judgment from performance and retrospective self-deception.

### Movement I
- Sela's trial produces an observation he cannot file cleanly.
- The Ledger forces him to explain the ordinary machinery of death and mercy.
- Merta and Col turn an old verdict back into a boy.
- He says Col's name when no one is present to hear it.
- He prays over an unknown grave without the name the rite requires.

### Movement II
- Perrin asks who checks the Tester.
- Tomas delays one burning until morning; the delay is the bend.
- Fen reveals how much the house knows through people it refuses to address.
- Harl's death proves that care, truth, and correct procedure do not prevent catastrophe.

### Movement III
- The house acts, records, and mythologizes faster than testing can contain.
- Wilda's confession exposes how much survives outside the Ledger.
- Tomas cannot close Sela's page.

### Visible bends
- His categories still function, but no longer comfort him.
- Syntax breaks when evidence cannot support a clean verdict.
- The blank page becomes an act rather than an absence.

### End-state
He remains the Tester. This is not restoration. It is a man choosing to continue inside a mechanism he no longer mistakes for certainty.

### Carry-forward scars
- Col's name.
- The delayed burning.
- The unknown grave.
- Perrin's question.
- Sela's unclosed page.

---

## ALAINE — architecture under pressure

### Small want
To have one true sentence received without losing the authority built from silence.

### Baseline
- About sixty; fifty years of authority.
- Has never heard.
- Built holiness from stillness, borrowed phrases, and the direction of a chair.
- Authored the burn-rule in the first year after being raised as Mother.

### Interlude I — Chapter 3
- Sela sits where Alaine never dared.
- Alaine cannot establish whether her own head turned before or after Sela.
- The old architecture first becomes uncertain.

### Interlude II — Chapter 17
- Perrin's method threatens the safety of an uncompared archive.
- Alaine confronts the fact that records can be laid beside one another.
- Procedure continues to replace confession.

### Interlude III — Chapter 26
- Sela prepares to leave every room Alaine remained inside.
- Alaine does not give the order that would stop her.
- The omission may be mercy, fear, love, self-preservation, or fatigue. The book does not choose.

### Visible bends
- Minimal by design.
- Her habits confess more than her thoughts.
- She permits one event she would previously have controlled.

### End-state
Still Mother. Still unconfessed. No longer able to believe that control and protection are identical.

### Carry-forward scars
- The half-breath.
- The grave and its vigil.
- Tomas's unclosed page.
- Sela's departure.
- The knowledge that the house may survive her truth but her authority may not.

---

## FEN — recognition purchased with information

### Small want
To hear his name spoken in public without the speaker having to hide.

### Baseline
- Twenty-four; tested at nineteen; five years Unwritten.
- Niche-tender, keeper of the wall, worker whose labor is acknowledged without a grammatical subject.
- Humor is labor and defense.

### Movement I
- Appears through Sela's friendship and the house's seating rules.
- His exclusion is kind, ordinary, and therefore durable.

### Movement II
- First POV access begins with an excuse he tells himself.
- Nessa preserves his name privately while public law erases it.
- Perrin's interest feels different from pity.
- Fen trades knowledge of the wall, the Ledger, and the house's habits for recognition.
- Merta refuses the corrected entry and identifies the violence inside Perrin's offer.
- Harl's body comes to Fen because the work always comes to the person the house cannot name.

### Movement III
- Fen's hurt becomes public conflict.
- His demand for his name exposes Whitehart's reliance on the people it grammatically removes.
- He becomes more capable of using information intentionally.
- He considers confessing to Sela and does not.

### Visible bends
- Jokes fail more often when the stakes rise.
- Pain simplifies his language.
- Usefulness becomes leverage.
- He learns that being seen can be bought and therefore can be sold.

### End-state
More visible, less innocent, still Unwritten. He loves Sela and withholds the fact most likely to end that love.

### Carry-forward scars
- Nessa's private use of his name.
- Perrin's attention.
- Merta's judgment.
- Harl's body.
- Sela's departure without the truth.

# 4. SUPPORTING ARCS

## Merta
A fixed moral witness whose meaning changes for the reader. She begins as a grieving mother the house tolerates and becomes the person most capable of seeing how mercy, testing, and history reproduce the same violence. Her refusal of Perrin is an act of protection for Sela and fidelity to Col.

## Wilda
Begins as correct law embodied. Her confession that she let Col go reveals that her legal certainty has always coexisted with one private refusal. She is neither absolved nor exposed as a hypocrite; she is a woman who chose once and then let silence become policy.

## Perrin
Begins as charming utility: he saves lives, listens well, and makes forgotten people legible. Merta forces him to see that a kind record can become a future weapon. He remains morally defensible because useful versions really can prevent harm. He remains dangerous because he decides which truth survives.

## Harl
Introduced through work, humor, pride, and care. His death chain is not prophecy fulfilled. Public shame, money, duty, the promised horse, the wet spring, and the safe neck all matter. His practical goodness and practical stupidity coexist.

## Ivet
Does not become malicious. Her sincere interpretive love becomes an engine that removes Sela's control over ordinary gestures. She demonstrates how myth can grow without a liar.

## Osric
Quick, capable, ambitious, and often correct. He sees how accounts move people. His arranged version after the gate is useful and partial. He is not a secret villain; he is an institutional talent discovering his medium.

## Ansel
Measures food, beds, capacity, and order faster than doctrine can respond. His private kindness keeps people alive while his stewardship also stabilizes the institution that erases Fen.

## Bryn Corr
Turns pilgrimage into beds, bread, bookings, and roofs. She makes Sela understand that unwanted authority has economic victims and beneficiaries.

## Orla
Applies the boundary literally. Her ruling is honorable, coherent, and ruinous. She carries Stonebear's philosophy without becoming a house lecture.

## Nella
Feeds the valley through credit, records, and moving goods. Her usefulness is real, her price is real, and her ledger makes Ravenshade morally attractive as well as dangerous.

## Roan and Ottar
Carry Stormrider into Book One at human scale. Roan brings contradictory Garron reports. Ottar carries the cost of Kavan without an explanatory dossier. Neither becomes a lore delivery device.

- Tomas: unclosed verdict.

# 6. READER-KNOWLEDGE RULES

- The reader knows Alaine's fraud; Sela and Tomas do not.
- The reader knows Fen's betrayal; Sela does not.
- No one, including the reader, knows whether the voice is supernatural.
- No one knows Col's fate.
- No one knows the grave's true origin.
- A future book may transfer one of these secrets only through a dramatized event and a canon update.

---

# 7. FUTURE-BOOK CAUTION

Do not reset these characters to their Book One baselines.

- Sela cannot return to believing that plain truth controls meaning.
- Tomas cannot return to uncomplicated trust in the rite.
- Alaine cannot unknow that Sela left because she did not command otherwise.
- Fen cannot return to innocent use of information.
- Merta cannot be reduced to grief alone after refusing Blackcrest.
- Perrin cannot forget Merta's explanation of his work.
